# Culturally Adaptable Examples of Evidence-Aligned Patterns

**Rule.** Examples illustrate food-group flexibility only. They do not imply genetic suitability by ancestry, ethnicity, or “traditional cuisine of origin.” Any pattern can be assembled from locally available, affordable foods that meet the same broad food-group and nutrient principles tested in trials. Verify nutrient adequacy and individual tolerances with a clinician or registered dietitian when needed. No commercial product endorsements.

## Mediterranean-aligned (food groups: vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, whole grains, olive or other liquid non-tropical oils, fish/poultry, limited red meat)

- Southern European baseline (as in PREDIMED/CORDIOPREV): tomatoes, leafy greens, chickpeas, lentils, whole-grain bread, almonds/walnuts, extra-virgin olive oil, sardines or other fish.
- East/Southeast Asian adaptation: leafy greens, bok choy, tofu or tempeh, brown rice or millet, peanuts or sesame, canola or peanut oil in moderation, fatty fish, limited processed meats.
- Latin American adaptation: beans, squash, tomatoes, avocado, corn tortillas (whole-grain where available), nuts/seeds, olive or avocado oil, fish.
- West African adaptation: leafy greens, okra, black-eyed peas or other legumes, millet or sorghum, groundnuts, fish, palm oil limited in favor of other unsaturated oils where feasible.

## DASH-aligned (food groups: vegetables, fruits, low-fat dairy or fortified alternatives, whole grains, nuts/legumes, lean proteins; sodium attention)

- North American baseline: broccoli, berries, low-fat yogurt or milk, oats, almonds, skinless poultry, herbs instead of salt.
- South Asian adaptation: dal (lentils), spinach or other greens, low-fat yogurt or buttermilk, whole-wheat roti, unsalted nuts, spices for flavor, limited added salt.
- Middle Eastern adaptation: cucumber, tomato, parsley, chickpeas, low-fat labneh or yogurt, bulgur or whole-wheat pita, olive oil in place of some butter, lemon and herbs.

## Portfolio-aligned (specific components: plant sterols, soy or other plant protein, viscous fiber sources, nuts; low saturated fat background)

- Core combination usable across cuisines: fortified plant-sterol spread or foods (where available), soy milk or tofu, oats/barley/psyllium or okra/eggplant for viscous fiber, a daily handful of nuts, on a background low in fatty meats and tropical oils.
- Example plate: oatmeal with soy milk and berries + almonds; lentil or soy-based main with vegetables; barley or oat side.

## Pescetarian / vegetarian / vegan-aligned

- Pescetarian: any of the above plant bases plus regular fish or seafood (e.g., salmon, mackerel, sardines, local white fish).
- Ovo-lacto vegetarian: plant base + eggs and/or dairy (yogurt, cheese, milk) in place of flesh foods.
- Vegan: plant base only; plan for reliable vitamin B12 source (fortified foods or supplement); attention to iron (legumes + vitamin C), omega-3 (flax, chia, walnuts, or algal sources), iodine and calcium as needed.

## Quality-focused low-fat aligned (vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean proteins; limited added fats and refined sugars)

- Any cuisine that emphasizes steamed, grilled, or lightly cooked vegetables and grains, skinless poultry or legumes, fruit, and minimal added oils or fried foods. Contrast with ultra-processed “low-fat” snack foods that are high in refined carbohydrate.

## Practical notes (from AHA 2023 statement and trial experience)

- Top-aligned patterns (DASH, Mediterranean, pescetarian, vegetarian) are described as adaptable to personal preferences, cultural practices, and budgets.
- Adherence in trials improved with education, counseling, or free provision of key foods; real-world success depends on food access, cooking skills, cost, and social context.
- No pattern requires foods from a single geographic region. Local, seasonal, and lower-cost equivalents that preserve the food-group balance are consistent with the evidence base.
