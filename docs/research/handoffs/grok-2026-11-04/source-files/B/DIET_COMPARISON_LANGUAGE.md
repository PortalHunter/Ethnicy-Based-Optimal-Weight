# Diet Comparison Language (Evidence-Strength Framing Only)

**Purpose.** Standardized, non-prescriptive wording for educational materials. Rank patterns by strength of evidence for specified endpoints, not by popularity or by any claim that one group of people is “made for” a cuisine. Flag unsupported claims.

## Evidence-strength ranking language (cardiometabolic endpoints)

Use only these or equivalent precise phrasings:

- “Among large primary randomized trials with hard cardiovascular events, the Mediterranean pattern has the most direct supportive data (revised PREDIMED primary prevention; CORDIOPREV secondary prevention), with the caveats of the 2018 reanalysis and population specificity.”
- “The DASH pattern has high-certainty short-term evidence for blood-pressure reduction under controlled feeding; hard-event evidence is indirect.”
- “The Portfolio pattern has high-certainty evidence for LDL-C and related lipid lowering in controlled trials of hyperlipidemic adults; hard-event evidence is absent.”
- “Vegetarian and pescetarian patterns show favorable associations with cardiovascular incidence in prospective cohorts and modest surrogate improvements in shorter trials; large hard-event RCTs are lacking.”
- “Quality-focused low-fat patterns showed similar average weight loss to quality-focused low-carbohydrate patterns in DIETFITS and did not reduce overall cardiovascular events versus usual diet in the Women’s Health Initiative; one secondary-prevention trial found them inferior to Mediterranean on composite events.”

Do **not** say:
- “X diet is the best for everyone.”
- “People of Y ancestry are genetically suited to Z cuisine.”
- “This pattern will replace your statin / blood-pressure medicine / diabetes medicine.”
- “You will lose exactly N kilograms” or “your risk will fall by N percent.”

## Hard outcome vs surrogate language

- Hard: “In the [trial], the rate of [MI/stroke/CV death/composite] was [absolute numbers or rates] with hazard ratio [HR (95% CI)].”
- Surrogate: “In controlled trials, [pattern] lowered [LDL-C/BP/weight] by [MD or mean difference with CI]. This is a risk-factor change, not a count of clinical events.”

## PREDIMED-specific required language

Always note: “The 2013 PREDIMED report was retracted because of randomization irregularities. The 2018 republication presents revised estimates that do not rely solely on the assumption of perfect randomization for every participant.”

## Unsupported-claim flags (use when relevant)

- Individualized weight-change predictions based on genotype or insulin secretion: not supported by DIETFITS.
- Medication replacement or dose reduction: not authorized by any single diet trial.
- Ancestral or ethnic genetic matching to a cuisine: outside the evidence and contrary to instructions.
- Guaranteed nutrient adequacy on restrictive vegan patterns without planning for B12 (and potentially other nutrients): not supported.

## Sample comparative sentences (app-safe)

- “Large randomized trials in Spanish adults have linked Mediterranean patterns to fewer major cardiovascular events than reduced-fat or low-fat advice; results come with important caveats about randomization (PREDIMED) and generalizability.”
- “DASH plus lower sodium reduced blood pressure more than either change alone in a controlled feeding study; long-term heart-event effects have not been tested in a comparable trial.”
- “Adding plant sterols, soy, viscous fiber, and nuts to a low-saturated-fat diet lowered LDL cholesterol about 17% in a meta-analysis of controlled trials.”
- “Observational studies associate vegetarian patterns with lower heart-disease rates; randomized trials of hard events are still needed.”
- “In one year-long trial, average weight loss was similar on a healthy low-fat pattern and a healthy low-carbohydrate pattern, and genetic or insulin markers did not predict which worked better.”

## Disposition guide

- educational: primary RCT hard-event or high-certainty surrogate data with clear caveats.
- context_only: cohort associations, guideline alignment scores, or extrapolated estimates.
- withhold: any claim of personal outcome, medication change, or genetic-cuisine matching.
