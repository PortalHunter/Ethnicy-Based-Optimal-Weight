# Cardiometabolic Diet Pattern Review (Team B)

**Scope and rules.** Educational synthesis of Mediterranean, DASH, Portfolio, pescetarian, vegetarian/plant-forward, vegan, and quality-focused low-fat patterns. Primary randomized evidence prioritized over cohorts. Hard clinical events (MI, stroke, CV death, revascularization) distinguished from surrogates (LDL-C, ApoB, TG, A1c, BP, weight). No ancestral-genotype cuisine claims, no medication-replacement claims, no invented effect sizes. Original papers plus corrections inspected. Source verification date: 2026-11-04.

## Mediterranean pattern

**Definition (trial operationalization).** High in vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, whole grains, olive oil; moderate fish/poultry; limited red/processed meat and sweets. PREDIMED added free EVOO or nuts; CORDIOPREV used intensive dietitian counseling without free food mandate in the same way.

**Hard outcomes.**  
- Revised PREDIMED 2018 (primary prevention, n=7447, median 4.8 y): composite MI/stroke/CV death HR 0.69 (0.53–0.91) for Med+EVOO and 0.72 (0.54–0.95) for Med+nuts vs reduced-fat advice after propensity-score adjustment. Absolute event rates ~3.4–3.8% vs 4.4%. 2013 original retracted because of randomization deviations (household members, site assignment issues); revised analyses do not assume perfect randomization. Total mortality point estimates crossed 1.0 in published figure data. Early stopping.  
- CORDIOPREV 2022 (secondary prevention, n=1002, 7 y): composite events 87 vs 111; adjusted HRs 0.72–0.75 range favoring Mediterranean. Effect clearer in men. Single-center, predominantly male.

**Surrogates.** Multiple smaller trials and secondary analyses show improvements in lipids, BP, glycemic markers, and endothelial function; magnitude varies with adherence and background diet. These do not automatically translate to the absolute event reductions above.

**Nutrient adequacy, adherence, adaptability.** Can meet micronutrient needs when varied; free provision or intensive counseling improves adherence in trials. Culturally adaptable (see separate file) but trial populations were Spanish. Affordability depends on olive oil/nut access and local produce prices. Contraindications: none absolute; nut allergy or severe olive-oil intolerance require modification. Uncertainty: generalizability beyond high-risk Spanish adults; control-diet intensity differed across trials.

**Flag.** Literature does not support claims that Mediterranean pattern replaces indicated medications or produces a fixed individual weight or event reduction.

## DASH pattern

**Definition.** Emphasizes vegetables, fruits, low-fat dairy, whole grains, nuts, legumes, lean proteins; limited saturated fat, sweets, and (in sodium variant) sodium.

**Hard outcomes.** No large primary hard-event RCT of the DASH pattern itself identified that matches the scale of PREDIMED/CORDIOPREV. BP is the dominant tested endpoint.

**Surrogates.** DASH-Sodium 2001 feeding trial (n=412, 30-day periods): DASH + low sodium vs control + high sodium lowered SBP by 11.5 mm Hg (hypertensives) and 7.1 mm Hg (normotensives). Sodium reduction and DASH effects were additive. High internal validity under controlled feeding; free-living adherence and hard events not established by this design.

**Other considerations.** Nutrient-dense; low-sodium version may require label reading and cooking. Culturally adaptable via local produce and low-fat dairy equivalents. AHA 2023 statement scored DASH highest (100) for alignment with 2021 guidance. Uncertainty: long-term hard-event impact under real-world conditions remains extrapolated from BP and observational data.

## Portfolio pattern

**Definition.** Combination of recognized cholesterol-lowering components (plant sterols ~2 g/day, soy protein, viscous fiber, nuts) usually layered on a low-saturated-fat background (NCEP Step II).

**Hard outcomes.** No large hard-event RCT. Estimated 10-year CHD risk reduced in the 2018 meta-analysis of controlled trials, but this is a calculated surrogate.

**Surrogates.** 2018 meta-analysis (7 comparisons, 439 hyperlipidemic participants): LDL-C mean difference −0.73 mmol/L (95% CI −0.89 to −0.56; ~17% relative). Also lowered non-HDL-C, ApoB, TG, BP, CRP. No effect on HDL-C or weight. GRADE high for LDL-C. Earlier metabolic-ward and free-living trials (including 6-month parallel trial n=351) show similar direction; magnitude tracks adherence.

**Considerations.** Requires consistent intake of specific foods; cost and availability of plant sterols/soy/nuts vary. Generally adequate if varied; soy/nut allergies are contraindications. Does not replace pharmacotherapy in the literature; effect size on LDL is clinically meaningful but smaller and more adherence-dependent than high-intensity statins in head-to-head short trials.

## Pescetarian, vegetarian, vegan / plant-forward

**Definitions.** Pescetarian: plant foods + fish/seafood ± dairy/eggs. Ovo-lacto vegetarian: plant foods + eggs and/or dairy, no flesh. Vegan: no animal products. “Plant-forward” is broader and overlaps Mediterranean/DASH/Portfolio.

**Hard outcomes.** No large primary RCTs of pure vegetarian or vegan patterns on incident CV events were identified. Prospective cohort meta-analyses (e.g., 13 cohorts, >800 000 participants) report lower CVD and IHD incidence with vegetarian patterns (RR ~0.85 and ~0.79) but null for total stroke; residual confounding cannot be excluded. Vegan data are thinner.

**Surrogates.** RCTs of vegetarian/vegan patterns (often in higher-risk or diabetic participants, average ~6 months) show modest reductions in LDL-C, HbA1c, and weight. AHA 2023 scored pescetarian 92, ovo-lacto vegetarian 86, vegan 78 (notes potential B12 deficiency if unsupplemented).

**Adequacy and caveats.** Well-planned ovo-lacto and pescetarian patterns readily meet needs. Vegan patterns require reliable B12 (and attention to iron, omega-3, iodine, calcium in some cases). Adherence and cultural fit are high when traditional plant-heavy cuisines are used; lower when highly restrictive or expensive specialty foods are required. No evidence supports genetic “suitability” by ancestry.

## Quality-focused low-fat

**Definition.** Emphasis on vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean proteins, limited added fats/sugars (not the same as ultra-processed low-fat products). DIETFITS “healthy low-fat” targeted ~20 g fat initially then liberalized while keeping quality high.

**Hard outcomes.** Women’s Health Initiative Dietary Modification Trial (n=48 835 postmenopausal women, ~8 y): overall null for CHD, stroke, and CVD (HRs ~0.97–1.02). Some subgroup analyses (normotensive, no baseline CVD) suggested lower CHD, but overall trial is null and later reports note possible post-randomization medication confounding in higher-risk women. CORDIOPREV low-fat arm was inferior to Mediterranean on composite events.

**Surrogates / weight.** DIETFITS 2018 (n=609, 12 mo): weight loss −5.3 kg (healthy low-fat) vs −6.0 kg (healthy low-carb); difference 0.7 kg (95% CI −0.2 to 1.6), non-significant. No genotype-pattern or baseline insulin-secretion interaction. Both arms improved diet quality and reduced calories ~20%.

**Considerations.** Quality matters: replacing fat with refined carbohydrate differs from replacing with vegetables/whole grains. AHA scored low-fat 78. Nutrient adequacy is achievable. Real-world adherence varies; extreme very-low-fat patterns can limit nuts/oils that have supporting evidence in other trials.

## Cross-pattern summary (evidence strength only)

| Pattern              | Hard CV events (primary RCT)          | Key surrogates                  | Certainty notes                  |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Mediterranean       | Favorable (PREDIMED revised, CORDIOPREV) | Lipids, BP, glycemia           | Moderate after PREDIMED caveats |
| DASH                | Not directly tested at scale          | BP (strong short-term)         | High for BP; extrapolated for events |
| Portfolio           | Not tested                            | LDL-C, ApoB, BP, CRP (strong)  | High for LDL-C                   |
| Pescetarian/vegetarian | Cohort associations only           | LDL, weight, HbA1c (modest)    | Low-moderate (observational)    |
| Quality low-fat     | Null overall (WHI); inferior to Med in CORDIOPREV | Weight similar to low-carb (DIETFITS) | Moderate for null weight difference |

**Integration rule reminder.** No single record authorizes medication changes, genetic inference, or automatic personal outcomes. Discuss patterns with clinicians in the context of overall care, medications, and preferences. Team disagreements (if any from other groups) should be logged rather than erased; primary RCTs and current guideline alignment within their stated scope receive priority.
