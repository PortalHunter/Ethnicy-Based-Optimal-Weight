# CORRECTION_AND_RETRACTION_LOG.md
## Grok Team D — Documented Corrections, Retractions, and Errata Relevant to Dietary Pattern Claims
**Verification date**: 2026-11-04  
**Principle**: “No definitive evidence” ≠ “proven false.” Record what is documented; do not over-interpret.

### 1. Gardner et al. Keto-Med Trial (Am J Clin Nutr 2022)
- **Original**: Gardner CD et al. Effect of a ketogenic diet versus Mediterranean diet on glycated hemoglobin in individuals with prediabetes and type 2 diabetes mellitus: The interventional Keto-Med randomized crossover trial. Am J Clin Nutr. 2022;116:640–652. DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/nqac154. PMID: 35641199.
- **Correction**: Published Am J Clin Nutr 2022 Nov/Dec (issue 116(6):1904). DOI: 10.1093/ajcn/nqac279. PMID: 36349565.
- **Nature**: Figure 3 y-axis scale corrected from −0.4 to 0.2 to −40 to 20 to prevent misinterpretation of percentage changes as absolute percentages. Explicit statement: “No other values or data points within the figure have changed.”
- **Impact**: None on primary or secondary results, p-values, or conclusions. Clarification only.
- **Status**: Corrected; data intact.

### 2. PREDIMED Trial (N Engl J Med)
- **Original**: Estruch R et al. Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease with a Mediterranean Diet. N Engl J Med. 2013;368:1279–1290.
- **Action**: Retracted June 2018; concurrently republished with reanalysis.
- **Republished**: Primary Prevention of Cardiovascular Disease with a Mediterranean Diet Supplemented with Extra-Virgin Olive Oil or Nuts. N Engl J Med. 2018;378:e34.
- **Reason**: Protocol deviations in randomization (enrollment of household members assigned same diet without individual randomization; clinic-level rather than individual allocation at one site; inconsistent use of randomization tables at another). Affected approximately 14–21% of the 7447 participants depending on exact counting.
- **Reanalysis**: Statistical adjustment for clustering and sensitivity analyses excluding the affected participants. Primary composite outcome effect size remained similar in direction and approximate magnitude.
- **Impact**: Results direction preserved; residual methodological concerns noted by critics (Ioannidis et al., BMJ 2019). Multiple derivative publications also required corrections or expressions of concern.
- **Status**: Original retracted; corrected version is the current reference. Not “proven false,” but protocol integrity was compromised for a subset.

### 3. 2026 ACC/AHA Multisociety Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia
- **Original publication**: Blumenthal RS et al. JACC / Circulation, March 2026 (ahead of print). DOI examples: 10.1016/j.jacc.2025.11.016; Circulation 2026;153:e1154–e1276.
- **Corrections identified**:
  - Take-home message on ApoB testing: triglyceride threshold changed from >200 mg/dL to ≥150 mg/dL.
  - Figure references and minor naming (Peter Toth → Peter P. Toth).
  - Bile-acid sequestrant table: Colesevelam dose units (mg → g); dosing frequency wording for cholestyramine, colesevelam, colestipol.
- **Impact**: Editorial and clarifying. No reversal of core recommendations on PREVENT equations, LDL-C goals, Lp(a) testing, or overall management strategy.
- **Status**: Corrected online versions exist. Always cite current official text.

### 4. Other notes
- No full retraction of the Keto-Med primary results.
- DIETFITS (JAMA 2018) and DASH-Sodium (NEJM 2001) have no major retractions or data-altering corrections identified in this audit.
- Portfolio meta-analysis (Chiavaroli 2018) no major errata identified.

**Distinction**: The PREDIMED randomization issues represent a genuine protocol failure that required retraction; the Keto-Med figure correction and 2026 guideline corrections are clarifications without data alteration. Neither case equates to the underlying biological findings being “proven false.”
