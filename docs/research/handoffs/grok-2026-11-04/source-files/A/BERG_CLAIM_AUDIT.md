# BERG_CLAIM_AUDIT.md

Independent audit of 10 concrete, date-stamped public claims by Dr. Eric Berg, D.C. (chiropractor; not a medical doctor). Claims drawn from publicly available YouTube videos, blog posts, and support pages. For each: exact short quotation where possible, public timestamp/URL, material conflicts/product marketing notes, citations he supplies (if any), primary evidence supporting/contradicting, and disposition.

Berg markets Healthy Keto®, intermittent fasting programs, and nutritional supplements (Director of Dr. Berg Nutritionals). Separate any Berg-conducted work (none of primary RCTs identified in these claims) from studies he discusses. Credential or popularity does not validate or invalidate research findings. No branded products cited as supporting evidence.

## Claim 1
- exact_claim: "What really creates the clogged artery is high levels of insulin from consuming too many carbohydrates." / Insulin, not dietary fat or cholesterol, is the primary driver of arterial clogging.
- public_timestamp_URL: YouTube "Is Keto Dangerous? High Fat Ketogenic Diet & Clogged Arteries" ~2017-03-12 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HmJSw0x3Rl0 (and similar restatements)
- material_conflicts: Promotes Healthy Keto® and related content/supplements.
- citations_supplied: Mechanistic framing; no specific primary RCT cited in available transcript summary.
- primary_evidence: Insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia are associated with cardiometabolic risk (observational and mechanistic). However, RCTs and meta-analyses of ketogenic/low-carb diets frequently show LDL-C increases (e.g., Keto-Med +10% SEM 4%; broader literature). Hard CVD event data for long-term keto are limited. Mediterranean patterns have stronger RCT cardiovascular outcome data in some populations.
- disposition: overstated (mechanistic element supported; causal primacy over dietary fat quality/LDL particle effects and long-term events not established by the evidence reviewed)

## Claim 2
- exact_claim: "Most people’s cholesterol and LDL are lower on keto. Others are hyper responders... But this is temporary."
- public_timestamp_URL: YouTube "Stop Worrying About LDL & High Cholesterol on Keto Diet" ~2019-01-05 https://youtu.be/dWMaXJtoXBo
- material_conflicts: Same as above.
- citations_supplied: General references to particle size (NMR); no full primary citations in summary.
- primary_evidence: Meta-analyses and trials (including Keto-Med) commonly show LDL-C increases on ketogenic diets; TG decreases and HDL may increase. "Temporary" is not consistently supported beyond short-term data. Particle size shifts noted in some studies but do not negate LDL-C or ApoB associations with risk in major guidelines.
- disposition: overstated / contradicted in aggregate trial data

## Claim 3
- exact_claim: "By far the majority of the time when fasting you’ll see a drop in LDL and triglycerides. Even if you do see a temporary spike in triglycerides you’ll normally see a drop in LDL and a spike in HDL. ... Even when it’s high it’s not a problem."
- public_timestamp_URL: YouTube "Why Do You Get High Cholesterol After Intermittent Fasting?" ~2019-07-28 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bJ4S-0XJ1fA
- material_conflicts: Promotes intermittent fasting with keto.
- citations_supplied: None specific in available summary.
- primary_evidence: Short-term fasting/TRE can improve TG and sometimes other markers; NEJM 2022 TRE + calorie restriction showed no significant superiority over calorie restriction alone for weight or metabolic factors at 12 months. LDL responses vary; "not a problem" when elevated contradicts major lipid guidelines linking LDL-C/ApoB to atherosclerotic risk.
- disposition: overstated

## Claim 4
- exact_claim: "The ketogenic diet is NOT a high protein diet, it is a moderate-protein diet." / High protein is a myth as the cause of issues.
- public_timestamp_URL: YouTube "5 Big Keto Diet Myths That Are Dead Wrong" ~2019-05-03 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CcweqjpQs7I
- material_conflicts: Same.
- citations_supplied: None specific.
- primary_evidence: Standard ketogenic definitions are high-fat, moderate-protein, very-low-carb. This definitional claim is accurate relative to classic keto. Protein intake effects on satiety, muscle, and kidney disease are context-dependent (e.g., CKD cautions in guidelines).
- disposition: supported (definitional); context-only for safety implications

## Claim 5
- exact_claim: "Ketoacidosis is a dangerous side effect – of a diabetic type 1 and a diabetic type 2 that is on insulin." (Implying not a concern otherwise for Healthy Keto.)
- public_timestamp_URL: Same video as Claim 4 (~2019-05-03)
- material_conflicts: Same.
- citations_supplied: None.
- primary_evidence: Nutritional ketosis ≠ ketoacidosis. However, ADA 2026 and other guidance explicitly caution against very-low-carbohydrate/ketogenic patterns in people on SGLT2 inhibitors due to euglycemic ketoacidosis risk, and in other high-risk groups. Insulin-treated patients also require careful monitoring.
- disposition: overstated (narrowly true for classic DKA but incomplete regarding SGLT2i and other risks)

## Claim 6
- exact_claim: Combining keto and intermittent fasting "will make doing both keto and fasting easier" and "help you reach your health and weight loss goals much faster." Fasting has a more powerful impact than keto alone initially.
- public_timestamp_URL: YouTube "Fasting vs Keto: What's Better?" ~2020-03-02 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SI4zOHcdr5w
- material_conflicts: Promotes both approaches and related products.
- citations_supplied: Mechanistic (GIP, autophagy, genes).
- primary_evidence: Short-term studies show benefits of both for weight and some markers. Longer RCTs (e.g., NEJM TRE) do not show clear additive superiority when calorie intake is controlled. Adherence is individual. Autophagy claims in humans remain preliminary.
- disposition: preliminary / overstated

## Claim 7
- exact_claim: "If cholesterol remains high on keto, liver issues might be hindering cholesterol processing." Recommendations include eating ≥7 cups vegetables/day, purified bile salts, choline, niacin.
- public_timestamp_URL: Support page "What are the ways to reduce cholesterol levels while following a ketogenic diet?" updated ~2025-04-16 https://support.drberg.com/faqs/how-to-lower-your-cholesterol-on-keto
- material_conflicts: Explicitly markets bile salts and other supplements.
- citations_supplied: Mechanistic; no primary RCTs for the supplement stack in this context.
- primary_evidence: High vegetable intake is consistent with nutrient-dense patterns. Bile salts, choline, and niacin have specific uses but are not established first-line or proven solutions for keto-associated LDL elevation in RCTs. Niacin has known side-effect profile. LDL elevation on keto is documented in trials without requiring "liver issues" as the explanation.
- disposition: not assessable / overstated (product-linked); primary evidence limited

## Claim 8
- exact_claim: "Ketogenic diets have been associated with an increase in size and volume of LDL cholesterol particles, which is considered to reduce cardiovascular risk by decreasing atherogenicity." (Cited as supporting heart health even if total cholesterol rises.)
- public_timestamp_URL: Blog "How to Lower Cholesterol on Keto" ~2025 https://www.drberg.com/blog/how-to-lower-your-cholesterol-on-ketogenic-diet
- material_conflicts: Same; promotes Healthy Keto.
- citations_supplied: Unspecified study summary on particle size.
- primary_evidence: Some studies report larger LDL particles on low-carb diets. Major guidelines prioritize LDL-C or ApoB concentration over particle size alone for risk assessment. Keto-Med and other trials document absolute LDL-C increases. Particle size does not fully offset concentration-related risk in current evidence.
- disposition: overstated

## Claim 9
- exact_claim: "A high-carb intake, not saturated fat, is linked to elevated cholesterol and cardiovascular disease, which explains why a ketogenic diet can lower cholesterol levels and promote heart health."
- public_timestamp_URL: Blog "Does Keto Work or Fail?" ~2025-04-08 https://www.drberg.com/blog/does-keto-work
- material_conflicts: Same.
- citations_supplied: Reference to a Progress in Cardiovascular Diseases paper (details not fully verified in summary).
- primary_evidence: Dietary pattern quality matters. Saturated fat replacement with unsaturated fats or whole-food carbs has evidence for lipid improvement in many trials. Ketogenic diets frequently raise LDL-C. Mediterranean (higher in unsaturated fats, fiber) has stronger outcome data in some RCTs. "Can lower cholesterol" is not the consistent finding.
- disposition: contradicted in key trial data

## Claim 10
- exact_claim: "The best ways to fix insulin resistance: 1. Get on the Healthy Keto® diet [and intermittent fasting]." Insulin resistance is caused by chronic carbohydrate consumption and high insulin.
- public_timestamp_URL: YouTube "The Surprising Facts about Insulin You Never Knew" ~2023-02-25 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7tHijDSVQrI
- material_conflicts: Promotes branded Healthy Keto®.
- citations_supplied: Mechanistic framing.
- primary_evidence: Calorie restriction, weight loss, physical activity, and various dietary patterns (including Mediterranean, low-carb, higher-protein) can improve insulin sensitivity markers. No single pattern is proven uniquely superior long-term in all populations. ADA 2026 emphasizes individualization and multiple acceptable patterns. Very-low-carb has short-term glycemic benefits in some RCTs but adherence and LDL issues noted.
- disposition: overstated (elements supported; exclusivity and long-term uniqueness not established)

Summary dispositions: Majority of reviewed claims are overstated or contradicted by primary RCTs/meta-analyses and current guidelines regarding LDL-C responses, long-term superiority, and safety boundaries. Definitional claims about protein content are supported. Mechanistic points on insulin and TG are partially supported but extrapolated beyond the evidence. Conflicts of interest via supplement and program marketing are material for several claims.

All claims audited from publicly available sources as of 2026-11-04. No personalization or product endorsement.
