# KETO_FASTING_EVIDENCE.md

Independent systematic-evidence review of defined ketogenic diets (typically ≤50 g carbohydrate/day or verified nutritional ketosis), nonketogenic low-carbohydrate diets, time-restricted eating/alternate-day fasting, and food-quality variants. Compared primarily against Mediterranean and usual-care diets in adults with elevated A1c, type 2 diabetes/prediabetes, high triglycerides, dyslipidemia, or obesity.

All records follow the required format. Higher priority given to well-conducted primary randomized evidence and current consensus/guidelines within scope. Contrary findings preserved. No source authorizes medical device claims, medication changes, dietary prescriptions, or automatic personal outcomes.

## Record 1: Keto-Med RCT
- claim_id: KetoMed_HbA1c_primary
- exact_claim: Well-formulated ketogenic diet (WFKD) vs Mediterranean-plus (Med-Plus) produced no significant difference in HbA1c percentage change at 12 weeks in adults with prediabetes or T2DM; both improved from baseline. WFKD lowered triglycerides more and raised LDL-C more.
- diet_definition: WFKD (well-formulated ketogenic, avoiding legumes/fruits/whole intact grains, nonstarchy vegetables emphasized, no added sugars/refined grains); Med-Plus (similar but including legumes, fruits, whole intact grains). Both low-carbohydrate relative to usual.
- population_definition: Adults ≥18 years with prediabetes or T2DM; n=40 randomized, primary analysis n=33 with complete data.
- study_type: Randomized crossover interventional trial
- comparison: WFKD vs Med-Plus (12 weeks each, random order)
- sample_size: 40 randomized / 33 complete
- followup: 12 weeks per diet phase
- outcome: Primary: % change HbA1c. Secondary: lipids, weight, nutrients, CGM.
- effect_size_and_CI: HbA1c: no significant between-diet difference. TG: WFKD -16% (SEM 4%) vs Med-Plus -5% (SEM 6%). LDL-C: WFKD +10% (SEM 4%) vs Med-Plus -5% (SEM 5%). Weight: ~8% vs ~7% (order interaction). Fiber and several nutrients lower on WFKD.
- p_value_if_relevant: TG P=0.02; LDL P=0.01
- primary_DOI_PMID_URL: 10.1093/ajcn/nqac154 PMID 35641199 NCT03810378
- errata_status: Correction published (10.1093/ajcn/nqac279) for Figure 3 y-axis scale (from -0.4–0.2 to -40–20 to clarify % change); no data values changed.
- risk_of_bias: Crossover design; some diet × order interactions; COVID-19 affected some weight data (self-report).
- quality_of_evidence: Moderate (small sample, short duration, open-label).
- limits_and_harms: Elevated LDL-C on WFKD; lower fiber and nutrient intakes; Med-Plus more sustainable at follow-up. Shared elements (nonstarchy vegetables, avoid added sugars/refined grains) likely drove HbA1c improvement.
- transportability: Adults with prediabetes/T2DM; not generalizable to children, pregnancy, advanced CKD, or those on SGLT2 inhibitors without monitoring.
- source_verified_on: 2026-11-04
- app_safe_statement: In one 12-week crossover trial, a well-formulated ketogenic pattern and a Mediterranean-style lower-carb pattern produced similar HbA1c improvements; the ketogenic pattern lowered triglycerides more but raised LDL cholesterol and provided fewer nutrients from legumes, fruit, and whole grains.
- no_personal_inference: true
- cross_team_dependencies: Aligns with ADA 2026 noting short-term carb restriction benefits and long-term uncertainties; lipid effects relevant to cardiovascular teams.
- recommended_disposition: educational

## Record 2: BMJ 2021 Low-Carbohydrate Meta-analysis
- claim_id: BMJ2021_LCD_remission
- exact_claim: Low-carbohydrate diets associated with higher rates of diabetes remission (HbA1c <6.5%) at 6 months versus control diets in T2DM; effects attenuated at 12 months and with stricter remission definition.
- diet_definition: LCD <130 g/day or <26% of energy from carbohydrate; VLCD <10% energy from carbohydrate; ≥12 weeks.
- population_definition: Adults with type 2 diabetes; 23 RCTs, 1357 participants.
- study_type: Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- comparison: LCD/VLCD vs control diets
- sample_size: 23 trials, 1357 participants (remission analysis 8 studies, n=264)
- followup: 6 and 12 months
- outcome: Diabetes remission (HbA1c <6.5%), weight, lipids, adverse events.
- effect_size_and_CI: 6-month remission: 76/133 (57%) vs 41/131 (31%); risk difference 0.32 (95% CI 0.17 to 0.47); I²=58%. Smaller non-significant effect for remission without medication. No clinically important adverse event or lipid differences at 6/12 months in aggregate.
- p_value_if_relevant: Not primary in abstract
- primary_DOI_PMID_URL: 10.1136/bmj.m4743 PMID 33441384
- errata_status: None identified
- risk_of_bias: 40.6% of outcomes judged low risk of bias; heterogeneity present.
- quality_of_evidence: Moderate to low certainty (GRADE-style implied)
- limits_and_harms: Debate on remission definition; long-term efficacy, safety, and dietary satisfaction uncertain. No major between-group adverse event differences reported.
- transportability: T2DM adults; shorter-term data stronger than ≥1 year.
- source_verified_on: 2026-11-04
- app_safe_statement: A 2021 meta-analysis of trials found low-carbohydrate diets linked to higher short-term (6-month) diabetes remission rates in type 2 diabetes, with more limited evidence beyond one year and ongoing questions about long-term safety and adherence.
- no_personal_inference: true
- cross_team_dependencies: Complements VLC meta; supports short-term glycemic discussion but not automatic superiority claims.
- recommended_disposition: educational

## Record 3: 2022 Very-Low-Carb Meta-analysis
- claim_id: VLC2022_HbA1c
- exact_claim: Very-low-carbohydrate/ketogenic diets may reduce HbA1c and triglycerides in prediabetes/T2D, but evidence of advantage over other strategies is limited at 12 months.
- diet_definition: VLC/KD ≤50 g carbohydrate or ≤10% total energy from carbohydrate per day.
- population_definition: Adults with prediabetes or T2D; 8 RCTs ≥6 months, 606 participants.
- study_type: Systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs
- comparison: VLC/KD vs control diets with higher carbohydrate
- sample_size: 8 trials, 606 participants
- followup: Primarily 12 months (primary outcome)
- outcome: HbA1c at 12 months; triglycerides, HDL.
- effect_size_and_CI: HbA1c analyses mixed: fixed-effects estimate VLC/KD minus control 0.01% (-0.22 to 0.25), p=0.91 in one set; -0.65% (-0.99 to -0.31), p<0.001 in others. TG: -0.28 mmol/L (-0.44 to -0.11), p<0.001. HDL higher in some reports.
- p_value_if_relevant: As above
- primary_DOI_PMID_URL: 10.1111/dom.14837 PMID 36064937
- errata_status: None identified
- risk_of_bias: Limited number of qualifying long-duration trials.
- quality_of_evidence: Limited certainty of superiority
- limits_and_harms: Authors conclude more well-designed studies needed. Broader literature notes frequent LDL-C increases.
- transportability: Prediabetes/T2D adults; not for high-risk groups without supervision.
- source_verified_on: 2026-11-04
- app_safe_statement: A 2022 meta-analysis of longer trials found very-low-carbohydrate diets may lower triglycerides and sometimes HbA1c in prediabetes or type 2 diabetes, but clear long-term advantage over other approaches is not established.
- no_personal_inference: true
- cross_team_dependencies: Consistent with ADA 2026 short-term vs long-term distinction.
- recommended_disposition: educational

## Record 4: NEJM Time-Restricted Eating Trial
- claim_id: NEJM_TRE_2022
- exact_claim: Among adults with obesity, time-restricted eating (8:00–16:00) plus calorie restriction did not produce significantly greater weight loss or metabolic improvements than daily calorie restriction alone at 12 months.
- diet_definition: Time-restricted eating window 8 hours (8am–4pm) with calorie restriction vs daily calorie restriction without time restriction. Both: 1500–1800 kcal/day men, 1200–1500 kcal/day women.
- population_definition: Adults with obesity; n=139 randomized.
- study_type: Randomized parallel-group trial
- comparison: TRE + calorie restriction vs daily calorie restriction
- sample_size: 139 randomized / 118 (84.9%) completed
- followup: 12 months
- outcome: Primary: change in body weight. Secondary: waist, BMI, body fat, lean mass, BP, metabolic risk factors.
- effect_size_and_CI: Weight: TRE -8.0 kg (95% CI -9.6 to -6.4); daily CR -6.3 kg (-7.8 to -4.7); difference -1.8 kg (-4.0 to 0.4).
- p_value_if_relevant: P=0.11 for weight difference
- primary_DOI_PMID_URL: 10.1056/NEJMoa2114833 PMID 35443107 NCT03745612
- errata_status: Correspondence published; no major data errata identified
- risk_of_bias: High completion rate; both arms calorie-restricted and attention-matched.
- quality_of_evidence: High (large, well-conducted RCT)
- limits_and_harms: No substantial adverse event differences. Results specific to calorie-restricted context in obesity; other TRE protocols differ.
- transportability: Adults with obesity on calorie restriction; may not apply to ad libitum or different windows.
- source_verified_on: 2026-11-04
- app_safe_statement: In a 12-month trial of adults with obesity, adding an 8-hour time-restricted eating window to calorie restriction did not significantly improve weight loss or metabolic measures beyond calorie restriction alone.
- no_personal_inference: true
- cross_team_dependencies: Relevant to intermittent fasting discussions; does not support automatic superiority claims.
- recommended_disposition: educational

## Record 5: ADA Standards of Care 2026 §5
- claim_id: ADA2026_nutrition
- exact_claim: No single ideal macronutrient distribution; variety of eating patterns acceptable including Mediterranean and carbohydrate-restricted. Reducing overall carbohydrate intake has evidence for improving glycemia. Very-low-carbohydrate patterns not currently recommended in pregnancy, lactation, children, kidney disease, or eating disorder risk; avoid with SGLT2 inhibitors due to ketoacidosis risk. Mediterranean has strong cardiovascular evidence.
- diet_definition: Individualized; emphasize nonstarchy vegetables, whole fruits, legumes, whole grains, nuts/seeds, low-fat dairy; minimize red meat, SSB, sweets, refined grains, ultraprocessed foods. Carb restriction as one option.
- population_definition: People with type 1/2 diabetes, prediabetes, gestational diabetes.
- study_type: Evidence-based clinical practice guideline
- comparison: Synthesis of RCTs, meta-analyses, observational data vs usual care or alternative patterns
- sample_size: N/A (guideline)
- followup: N/A
- outcome: Glycemic control, weight, lipids, cardiovascular outcomes, safety.
- effect_size_and_CI: References short-term benefits of carb restriction for A1C (<6 months stronger); cites Keto-Med and longer meta-analyses showing limited long-term glycemic advantage.
- p_value_if_relevant: N/A
- primary_DOI_PMID_URL: 10.2337/dc26-S005
- errata_status: Current 2026 edition (released ~Dec 2025)
- risk_of_bias: N/A (guideline process)
- quality_of_evidence: A/B ratings for key recommendations (e.g., Mediterranean cardiovascular A; carb reduction B)
- limits_and_harms: Explicit cautions for VLC in high-risk groups and SGLT2i users; nutrient adequacy monitoring during weight loss; long-term data gaps.
- transportability: Broad diabetes population; individualization required.
- source_verified_on: 2026-11-04
- app_safe_statement: Current diabetes guidelines note that lower-carbohydrate eating patterns can help some people improve blood glucose in the short term and that Mediterranean-style patterns have strong supporting evidence; they also flag specific situations where very-low-carbohydrate approaches are not recommended and advise discussing any major dietary change with a healthcare professional.
- no_personal_inference: true
- cross_team_dependencies: Integrates findings from the RCTs and meta-analyses above; safety cautions cross-link to medication and special-population teams.
- recommended_disposition: educational

Additional notes: Adherence beyond one year is commonly lower for very-low-carbohydrate patterns. Nutrient adequacy (fiber, certain micronutrients) requires attention. No source here supports automatic personalization from ancestry or lab-derived ideal weights.
