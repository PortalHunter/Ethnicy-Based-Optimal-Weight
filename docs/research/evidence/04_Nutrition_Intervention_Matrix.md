# Research Team 4 — Nutrition Intervention Matrix

**Research draft v0.9 | Evidence current through 2026-11-03 | No clinical publication approval.** Link all advice candidates to the numeric measurement, its actual clinical context, and a source claim in the [LAB evidence ledger](04_Laboratory_Evidence_Matrix.md). Distinguish improvement of a **surrogate measurement** from a reduction in clinically important events. No specific supplement or nutrient goal is a production recommendation without clinical governance.

**Disposition legend:** `Education candidate` = may explain evidence with qualifications, not prescribe; `Conditional` = withhold advice unless clinical context is sufficient; `No diet recommendation` = result alone is not actionable for nutrition.

## What magnitude of response is supported—and what is not?

- **Clinical outcome:** DPP (Knowler et al., 2002, randomized high-risk US adults) intensive lifestyle vs placebo reduced new type 2 diabetes by 58% relative, 95% CI 48–66% over mean 2.8 years; the *app* does not reproduce this high-contact intervention. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa012512
- **Clinical outcome (higher-risk cohort):** the **corrected and republished** PREDIMED trial (Estruch et al., 2018) found fewer major CV events with Mediterranean-style patterns and nuts or extra-virgin olive oil versus a reduced-fat control in selected high-risk Spanish participants; do not convert a dietary pattern into a Mediterranean *genotype* effect. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389
- **Lipid surrogate:** Chiavaroli et al., 2018, meta-analysis of seven controlled comparisons, n=439, Portfolio-pattern LDL-C difference −0.73 mmol/L, 95% CI −0.89 to −0.56, roughly −17%; no pooled body weight effect. **No individual predicted percent reduction.** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pcad.2018.05.004
- **Disease-specific liver outcomes:** EASL/EASD/EASO 2024 LoE2 weight-loss strata ≥5% fat, 7–10% inflammation, ≥10% fibrosis in overweight adults with MASLD; the guideline cautions that long-term hard liver outcomes remain less certain. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhep.2024.04.031
- **Blood pressure surrogate, not serum sodium:** Sacks et al., 2001, 412-person DASH–sodium feeding study: low-sodium DASH versus high-sodium control yielded mean systolic BP −7.1 mmHg without and −11.5 mmHg with hypertension. This **does not authorize** lowering salt to correct low serum sodium. https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM200101043440101

## Explicitly harmful shortcuts to block

1. Blanket “eat bananas” for low potassium or “avoid bananas” for high potassium: renal disease, medication and acute cardiac risk are unknown.
2. “Stop all carbohydrates” for HbA1c: unsafe with insulin/secretagogues and can be particularly dangerous with SGLT2 inhibitors and ketoacidosis risk; see ADA 2026 recommendation 5.26.
3. Iron supplements from low MCV or vegetarian ancestry; high ferritin may reflect inflammation and thalassemia carriers are not automatically iron deficient.
4. “Take vitamin D because of skin color,” “take chromium/magnesium for diabetes,” “use iodine for abnormal TSH,” or “drink alcohol to raise HDL”: unsupported and potentially harmful.
5. Diet or weight loss as sole response to severe LDL, severe triglycerides, kidney/electrolyte abnormalities or signs of liver dysfunction.
