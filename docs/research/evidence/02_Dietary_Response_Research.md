# Research Team 2 — Dietary Response Research
**Evidence review | 2026-11-01 | Explicitly includes unfavorable and null findings**

## Principal answer

A person can experience a large, reproducible benefit from ketogenic eating without that benefit being attributable to ancestry or genotype. Four explanations must be distinguished: **treatment effect in that individual**, behavior/adherence change, baseline phenotype (e.g., elevated HbA1c), and **true genotype-by-diet interaction**. Only the last supports genetic diet matching. The robust randomized evidence is not sufficient to label someone “genetically suited to keto.” [DIETFITS, KETOMED, FTO, APOA2]

### 1. Ketogenic diet: benefit and nonbenefit must both be visible

**Definition control.** Do not conflate: ketogenic (<20–50 g carbohydrates/day in many clinical protocols), very-low-carbohydrate (<26% calories in some reviews), and moderate low carbohydrate. Record actual grams, % energy, ketone measurement if any, fiber, saturated/unsaturated fat mix, calories, protein and adherence. A person reporting “keto” may have followed a very different diet from an intervention group.

**Glycemia:** reducing digestible carbohydrates often lowers postprandial excursions and short-term HbA1c in adults with T2D. The size of the effect depends on baseline hyperglycemia, dietary energy balance, weight change, drugs, food choices, coaching and duration. ADA 2026 supports considering carbohydrate reduction *for some adults*, not prescribing keto universally. Keto–Med directly shows similarly improved HbA1c across two high-quality, low-sugar diets at 12 weeks [ADA26, KETOMED].

**Weight:** marked short-term weight loss is possible, but controlled long-term superiority across well-supported patterns is not established. DIETFITS shows broadly comparable one-year loss across low-carb and low-fat; note the low-carb arm was not chronically ketogenic. Isocaloric conditions, protein, adherence and comparator quality should be prespecified in future trials [DIETFITS].

**Triglycerides:** lower carbohydrates can improve elevated triglycerides in some patients; this finding does not by itself demonstrate reduced cardiovascular events. LDL and apoB should be measured separately [KETOMED, LIPID26].

**LDL/apoB:** response is heterogeneous and sometimes adverse. Keto–Med's LDL difference is not a minor technicality. A person can have improved TG/HbA1c and worsened atherogenic lipoproteins at the same time. Different fat sources matter. A known FH diagnosis or substantially elevated LDL-C is a reason for clinician-managed decisions rather than default high-saturated-fat keto [KETOMED, LIPID26].

**Diet quality and deficiencies:** some restrictive keto implementations exclude legumes, fruit, whole grains and fiber-rich foods without a medically demonstrated need. Monitor fiber, micronutrient adequacy, diet satisfaction, social feasibility and possible constipation; individual nutrient risk depends on actual food choices, not the label alone [ADA26, KETOMED].

**Long-term outcomes:** avoid claiming superior reductions in myocardial infarction, stroke or mortality from keto. Available high-quality trials emphasize intermediate markers, short duration or selected participants. At minimum, cardiovascular hard-endpoint equivalence is unproven; informed counseling should acknowledge this.

### 2. Conditions in which a “keto success” narrative can be unsafe

- **FAO disorders (MCAD, LCHAD/TFP, related):** fatty-acid fuel handling is impaired; fasting and high-fat low-carbohydrate approaches may provoke metabolic crisis. **Do not recommend**, refer to metabolic specialists [MCAD, LCHAD].
- **SGLT2 treatment:** guideline flag for ketoacidosis risk, route to prescriber/Team 3; no autonomous medication adjustments [ADA26].
- **Insulin or other glucose-lowering drugs:** marked carb change can require supervised drug review to prevent hypoglycemia [ADA26].
- **Pregnancy, lactation, children, CKD, or eating-disorder risk:** ADA advises against routine very-low-carb prescriptions; individualized clinical assessment [ADA26].
- **FH, severe hypercholesterolemia, marked keto-related LDL/apoB rises:** no one-size keto recommendation; evaluate clinical risk and better-tolerated patterns [LIPID26].
- **PAH deficiency/PKU:** generic high-protein diets conflict with condition-specific amino acid management [PKU24].
- **HFI:** specialist-confirmed restriction differs substantially from ordinary fructose sensitivity; general keto menus are not a safe disease-management substitute [HFI].

### 3. What genotype predictors do and do not say

**DIETFITS:** primary prespecified genotype-by-treatment interaction null (p=0.20) and baseline insulin secretion null (p=0.47); apply conclusion only to its genotype classifier, participants and diets. **FTO:** IPD meta-analysis n=9,563 showed no response modification across interventions. **APOA2:** a 2025 exploratory/post hoc stratification of DIETFITS linked rs5082, saturated-fat threshold and different weight-loss responses in a subset maintaining criteria across visits. It does not overturn the original primary finding: subgroup filtering, multiple comparisons, choice of saturated fat threshold and lack of prospective replication restrict external validity [DIETFITS, FTO, APOA2].

**AMY1:** gene copy number can correlate with postprandial glucose biology, but inconsistent obesity associations and measurement complexity undermine a consumer rule for allowable starch. **FADS1/FADS2:** biologic influence on PUFA metabolism is credible; a genotype-to-diet strategy improving clinical outcomes is not established. **T2D PRS:** stratifies risk to varying degrees and has portability limitations; it is not a carbohydrate-tolerance test [AMY26, PRS25, PRS26].

### 4. Measurable individual response outperforms speculative diet prediction (proposed hierarchy)

1. **Safety / established diagnosis:** potential contraindications and specialized nutrition needs.
2. **Current phenotype:** HbA1c/fasting glucose, LDL-C/non-HDL-C or apoB, triglycerides, blood pressure, eGFR, relevant micronutrient/iron markers, weight/waist, symptoms.
3. **Documented dietary exposure and response:** actual diet pattern, calories, fat quality, fiber, medication stability, sleep/activity and adherence; time series over 8–12 weeks, then 6–12 months.
4. **Clinically evaluated family history:** triggers screening / referral, not diet restriction.
5. **Genotype/PRS:** as clinician-interpreted safety or disease diagnostic evidence only, or clearly labeled research stage when predictive value is unvalidated.
6. **Self-reported ancestry:** epidemiologic context and equity monitoring, not dietary prescription.

A single HbA1c reading can be misleading because of medication adjustment and confounding. Clinician-supervised *n-of-1* diet trials may estimate personal tolerability and biomarker response (particularly reversible outcomes), but are not proof of genotype interaction and cannot measure rare cardiovascular events. Standardize home BP technique, fasting/nonfasting lipids, lab variability, weight trends and report adverse effects.

### 5. Practical example: two people both do well on keto

A: HbA1c 7.8% → 6.5%, TG 250 → 130 mg/dL, LDL 110 → 105 with well-documented carb reduction and medication stability. B: HbA1c 7.8% → 6.5%, TG 250 → 130, LDL 110 → 240, apoB markedly higher. **These values are hypothetical, not trial observations.** Both improved glycemia and TG, but the clinically appropriate conversations differ sharply. Do not award a “keto compatibility genotype” or average their responses. Treat adverse lipid change seriously [LIPID26].

### 6. Design for a controlled predictive trial

Randomize by dietary approach with balanced counseling and measured food quality, not by genotype; test a **pre-registered genotype × randomized-diet interaction** for prespecified outcomes. To test utility, compare genotype-informed choice against phenotype-informed standard choice in a second trial. Primary endpoint ideally 12-month HbA1c or LDL/apoB depending on population; weight and adherence secondary; report the full intention-to-treat sample and absolute treatment differences. If using multiple variants use independent external replication and correction for multiplicity. Never calibrate and test on the same people.
