# Parallel Grok research prompts — diets and ancestry-related biomarkers

**Purpose:** independent research workstreams for Ancestry Health. Use four separate Grok sessions or agents **in parallel**; each team must produce independent evidence, not repeat or defer to other teams. The integration team merges only after source checks.

**Shared background for every prompt:** Free, open educational website [Ancestry Health](https://github.com/PortalHunter/Ethnicy-Based-Optimal-Weight); ideal-weight math must not be touched. Optional browser-local lab entry currently provides narrow source-linked explanations for A1c, glucose, LDL-C, triglycerides and kidney context. We want **research-based dietary patterns to discuss with doctors**, not automated diagnoses, personal medical advice, treatment, drug changes, ancestral genotype inference, new weight equations or commercial product endorsements. As of 2026-11-04, inspect original papers plus corrections/errata; distinguish RCTs from cohorts, mechanistic, preprints, anecdotes and marketing. **Do not invent DOIs, sample counts, effect sizes or recommendations.** Record denominators and CI for all estimates, follow-up and certainty, conflicts of interest and whether outcomes are hard clinical events or surrogate biomarkers.

## Grok Team A — Keto / low carb / intermittent fasting, with a Dr. Berg claim audit

> Act as an independent systematic-evidence reviewer, not a ketogenic-diet advocate or opponent. Review defined ketogenic diets (usually ≤50 g carbs/day or otherwise verified nutritional ketosis), nonketogenic low-carbohydrate diets, time-restricted eating/alternate-day fasting, and food-quality variants. Compare against Mediterranean and usual-care diets in adults with high A1c, type 2 diabetes/prediabetes, high triglycerides, dyslipidemia or obesity. Start with Keto-Med RCT (Gardner et al., AJCN 2022 DOI 10.1093/ajcn/nqac154 and its correction 10.1093/ajcn/nqac279), BMJ 2021 low-carbohydrate meta-analysis (PMID 33441384), 2022 very-low-carb meta-analysis (PMID 36064937), ADA Standards of Care 2026 §5 (10.2337/dc26-S005), and NEJM time-restricted eating trial (10.1056/NEJMoa2114833). Check current 2026 updates. Pay special attention to HbA1c primary endpoints, LDL increases, TG, nutrient adequacy, adherence beyond one year, SGLT2 ketoacidosis, insulin/secretagogue hypoglycemia, kidney disease, pregnancy, breastfeeding and eating disorders.
>
> Independently inspect **10 concrete, date-stamped claims from Dr. Eric Berg, D.C.** (or identify fewer when exact sources cannot be verified) covering Healthy Keto, insulin resistance, fasting, cholesterol, supplementation and metabolic health. For every claim record the exact short quotation, public timestamp and URL, material conflicts/product marketing, citations he supplies, primary evidence supporting/contradicting it, and a disposition: supported / supported only in a narrow population / preliminary / overstated / contradicted / not assessable. Do not infer that a speaker's credential or popularity validates or invalidates any research finding. Separate Berg's own trials from studies he discusses. Avoid citing branded products as supporting evidence.
>
> Deliver `KETO_FASTING_EVIDENCE.md`, `BERG_CLAIM_AUDIT.md`, `KETO_SAFETY_CASES.md`, and `KETO_SOURCES.csv` with trial identifiers, study design, actual participant criteria, all outcomes and CIs, study limits, corrections, a counterevidence section, and safe one-sentence education copy for an app. Include 15 synthetic edge cases where generic keto/fasting suggestions must be suppressed. No personalization from self-reported ancestry.

## Grok Team B — Mediterranean, DASH, Portfolio, vegetarian/plant-forward and high-quality low-fat

> Independently evaluate Mediterranean, DASH, Portfolio, pescetarian, vegetarian, vegan, quality-focused low-fat and culturally adapted traditional dietary patterns. Start with the **revised 2018 PREDIMED publication** (10.1056/NEJMoa1800389; explicitly account for the retracted 2013 original and randomization problems), CORDIOPREV 2022 (PMID 35525255), DASH-Sodium 2001 (10.1056/NEJM200101043440101), Portfolio controlled-trial meta-analysis 2018 (PMID 29807048), DIETFITS 2018 (10.1001/jama.2018.0245), and AHA 2023 dietary-pattern statement (PMID 37128940). Search for the latest large primary RCTs, long-term cohorts as secondary context, corrected guidelines, and adverse effects.
>
> For each dietary pattern compare the actual **hard outcomes** (events, mortality, disease incidence) against **surrogates** (LDL, ApoB, TG, A1c, blood pressure, body weight), plus nutrient adequacy, cultural adaptability, accessibility, food affordability, real-world adherence, contraindications and uncertainty. Never infer one ethnicity is genetically "made for" a specific cuisine. Rank only **evidence strength for each specified endpoint**, not people's diets. Explicitly flag claims of individualized weight change or medication replacement that the literature does not validate.
>
> Deliver `DIET_PATTERN_TRIAL_MATRIX.csv`, `CARDIOMETABOLIC_DIET_REVIEW.md`, `CULTURALLY_ADAPTABLE_EXAMPLES.md`, and `DIET_COMPARISON_LANGUAGE.md`. Every row needs DOI/PMID/link, design, n, criteria, comparator, follow-up, effect + CI, correction status, certainty, harms and honest caveats.

## Grok Team C — Lab-linked nutrition guidance, safety, ancestry and equity

> Act as a combined laboratory medicine, dietetics and clinical-safety review team. Build a conservative applicability and **withholding** map for manually reported A1c, fasting vs random glucose, LDL, triglycerides, HDL, total cholesterol, potassium, reported eGFR and urine ACR. Test whether specific research-supported diet discussions can responsibly appear after a user selects a lab's high/low/critical flag, without creating a diagnosis or self-treatment plan. Use ADA Standards 2026, 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline **including June and September 2026 corrections**, KDIGO 2024, and primary diet trials. Distinguish physiological measurements, sample/assay quality, lab-specific reference flags, proven disease, genetic assay variants and self-identified ancestry. Watch for HbA1c hemoglobin-variant interference, race-free eGFR, measured vs ancestry-estimated Lp(a), and medication-related confounders.
>
> List **stop conditions** for general nutrition output: reported critical values, serious glucose concerns, severe triglycerides, abnormal potassium, flagged kidney tests, known pregnancy, SGLT2 use, insulin or sulfonylureas with strict carb restriction/fasting, kidney disease, eating disorder risk, and uncertain specimen/unit assumptions. Separate warnings that can be shown reliably from situations where medication/symptom history is simply *unknown*. Do not invent universal urgency cutoffs. Explicitly prevent the lab module from modifying the ideal-weight output.
>
> Deliver `LAB_TO_DIET_CLAIM_MAP.csv`, `CLINICAL_SAFETY_REVIEW.md`, `EQUITY_AND_TRANSPORTABILITY.md`, and `USER_COMPREHENSION_CASES.md` with at least 30 synthetic conflicting, misleading and incomplete lab panels, including mixed ancestry; and suggested neutral, patient-friendly language for clinician discussions.

## Grok Team D — Adversarial independent replication and synthesis

> Independently search current primary literature and **challenge** conclusions from Teams A–C. Do not accept their citations or the site's research notes as verified. For every proposed public-facing statement ask whether the exact source exists, matches its cited DOI, population, intervention, effect estimate, confidence interval, duration and outcome; check corrections, retractions, selection bias, publication bias, funding and whether a significant surrogate means a clinical outcome. Verify the keto vs Mediterranean comparison, including November/December 2022 Figure 3 correction; revised PREDIMED; 2026 ADA nutrition recommendation 5.26 on SGLT2 inhibitors; DASH-Sodium BP effects; Portfolio LDL results; DIETFITS null genotype-by-diet effect; and 2026 lipid guideline corrections.
>
> Create an **evidence conflict register** with graded uncertainties and examples where an apparently desirable change in one lab marker can coexist with harm or uncertainty in another (e.g. TG down, LDL up). Investigate the strongest contrary trials for each approach and discuss the value/limits of Mendelian randomization and observational epidemiology. Explicitly identify claims that should be removed, down-rated, or confined to a comparison rather than a recommendation. No demographic group should receive a different recommendation without validated patient-level predictive evidence.
>
> Deliver `INDEPENDENT_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`, `CONTRADICTION_REGISTER.csv`, `CORRECTION_AND_RETRACTION_LOG.md`, and `SAFE_CANDIDATE_COPY.md`. Treat 'no definitive evidence' and 'proven false' as distinct conclusions; report any inaccessible sources rather than guessing.

## Format all teams must use

Every evidence record should include: `claim_id`, `exact_claim`, `diet_definition`, `population_definition`, `study_type`, `comparison`, `sample_size`, `followup`, `outcome`, `effect_size_and_CI`, `p_value_if_relevant`, `primary_DOI_PMID_URL`, `errata_status`, `risk_of_bias`, `quality_of_evidence`, `limits_and_harms`, `transportability`, `source_verified_on`, `app_safe_statement`, `no_personal_inference`, `cross_team_dependencies`, and `recommended_disposition` (`educational` / `context_only` / `withhold`).

**Integration rule:** no source record by itself authorizes medical device claims, medication changes, dietary prescriptions, genetic inference, lab-determined ideal weights or automatic personal outcomes. Team disagreements must be logged, not erased; give higher priority to well-conducted primary randomized evidence and current consensus/guidelines within their actual scope, while preserving contrary findings. These prompts are written for independent work, not for a single summarizer to assert completion of all four reports.
