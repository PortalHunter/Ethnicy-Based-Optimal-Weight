# 02 — High LDL-C/non-HDL-C with low TG and favorable HDL: apoB, NMR LDL-P and risk

**Core question:** If TG are low/normal, HDL is normal/high, and LDL-C/non-HDL-C remain high during carbohydrate restriction, how much does advanced lipoprotein testing add? **Answer:** apoB can materially clarify atherogenic particle burden; LDL-P is another particle-number measure. LDL particle *size* is generally less clinically actionable. The high-LDL/low-TG subgroup has little direct long-term ketogenic outcomes evidence, so favorable markers must not be used to override severe LDL/apoB assessment.

## 1. Distinguish cholesterol cargo from particle number

| Measurement | Analytic concept | Interpretation and limits |
|---|---|---|
| **LDL-C** | Cholesterol mass in the LDL fraction, in mg/dL or mmol/L | Most established clinical standard and treatment endpoint; calculated LDL-C depends on formula and TG distribution; may be high from many LDL particles or cholesterol-rich particles |
| **Non-HDL-C** | Total cholesterol minus HDL-C | Cholesterol in LDL, remnant, VLDL, IDL and Lp(a)-associated fractions; useful widely available secondary measure, but if TG/remnants are very low it often closely tracks LDL-C |
| **ApoB** | ApoB protein concentration, typically mg/dL, approximating one protein molecule per LDL, IDL, VLDL and Lp(a) particle | Captures atherogenic particle concentration across subclasses; analytic standardization good; not identical to LDL-P because it also includes VLDL/remnant and Lp(a) particles |
| **NMR LDL-P** | Nuclear magnetic resonance-estimated concentration of LDL/IDL-size particles, typically nmol/L | A second approach to particle number; platform definitions, sizes and reference ranges vary; not the same as total apoB |
| **LDL size / small LDL-P** | Distribution of LDL particle diameters and subclasses | Describes phenotype; associations confounded by apoB/LDL-P, TG and metabolic factors; no established safe “large particle” exception |
| **Lp(a)** | Concentration of an apoB-containing lipoprotein with apolipoprotein(a) | Genetically influenced independent risk marker, not inferred from TG or HDL; preferentially report molar concentration nmol/L without a universal conversion to mg/dL |
| **TG/HDL-C ratio** | Ratio of two metabolic lipid surrogates | Can correlate with insulin resistance in some cohorts; not a substitute for apoB or an absolute ASCVD risk model |

Each atherogenic particle contains one apoB-100 protein in LDL, IDL/VLDL, and Lp(a), while gut-derived particles have apoB-48; clinical apoB methods approximate total atherogenic particles. One LDL particle can carry variable cholesterol, so elevated LDL-C and apoB are correlated but not identical. [S18, S19, S20]

## 2. Why the low-TG/high-HDL ketogenic pattern is special but not automatically reassuring

Carbohydrate restriction often reduces TG and increases HDL-C. If LDL-C rises markedly, three distinct patterns could exist:

- **Cargo-dominant:** LDL-C rises more than apoB (larger, cholesterol-rich LDL). Particle burden might be lower than another person at the same LDL-C; absolute LDL exposure is not zero.
- **Particle-count dominant:** LDL-C **and** apoB rise (even if TG is low, HDL high and LDL particles large). The number of apoB-containing particles increases. [S04]
- **Mixed:** LDL-C, apoB and remnant/Lp(a) contributions vary; LDL-P versus apoB discordance requires checking the assay and lipoprotein classes.

A low TG/HDL ratio can indicate improved insulin sensitivity or fewer triglyceride-rich remnants. It neither directly measures endothelial lipoprotein retention nor establishes that a given long-term apoB concentration is safe. Similarly, a non-HDL-C increase is not entirely independent evidence when the entire increase is driven by LDL cholesterol.

## 3. Prospective evidence: why apoB/LDL-P discordance matters

### MESA LDL-P analysis — [S18]

Otvos and colleagues recruited from a cohort of 6,814 adults, with **5,598 eligible for the prospective LDL-C vs LDL-P discordance analyses**, and recorded 319 CVD events over about 5.5 years. Among participants whose LDL-C and LDL-P percentile rankings differed by ≥12 percentile units, risk associated with LDL-P more clearly than LDL-C: adjusted HR per SD 1.45 (95% CI 1.19–1.78) for LDL-P versus 1.07 (0.88–1.30) for LDL-C. This suggests that particle concentration can provide more relevant risk information than cholesterol cargo when discordant.

**Limits:** Population included white, Black, Hispanic and Chinese American participants, but not a Filipino-specific ketogenic subgroup; observational design and lipid definitions limit causal treatment conclusions. This is strong evidence about **risk prediction within measured discordance**, not proof NMR-directed therapy reduces events compared with apoB-guided or LDL-C-guided care. [S18]

### UK Biobank and expert consensus — [S19, S22]

The 2024 NLA apoB consensus reviewed a large body of observational and trial secondary evidence and argues that apoB more reliably describes particle burden when apoB and LDL-C disagree. Another UK Biobank analysis summarized by ACC examined nearly 294,000 adults, finding meaningful apoB variation at a fixed LDL-C or TG, associated with differing long-term ASCVD rates. Evidence supports apoB as valuable **in addition to** the ordinary lipid panel. [S19, S22]

**Risk attribution is not symmetric:** cholesterol-poor, numerous LDL particles are commonly encountered in insulin resistance. Ketogenic “cholesterol-rich” LDL can show the opposite discordance, so risk may be lower **than a person with equally high LDL-C and high apoB**. However, a relatively lower apoB is not proof of low *absolute* risk or lack of cumulative LDL exposure. There is no validated phenotype-specific formula to subtract “LMHR cholesterol” from cardiovascular risk.

### NMR LDL-P compared with apoB

ApoB measures all apoB-containing atherogenic particles, whereas NMR LDL-P estimates an LDL-focused subset. Their concentrations usually correlate, but discordance can arise from VLDL/remnant abundance, Lp(a), or assay/platform differences. Evidence does not support routine addition of LDL-size and subclass profiles for every person with high LDL-C once standard lipids, apoB, Lp(a) and clinical risk are already available. [S19, S23]

**Actionability test:** Before ordering NMR, specify *what result would change the clinical plan.* If the only sought result is “large buoyant LDL,” that does not neutralize elevated apoB. If LDL-C is extremely high, clinical evaluation is indicated regardless of whether an advanced panel shows large particles. [S20]

## 4. Example discordance scenarios (illustrative, not clinical cutoffs)

| LDL-C | ApoB | TG | What it suggests |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 190 mg/dL | 95 mg/dL | 55 mg/dL | More LDL cholesterol per apoB particle than someone with the same LDL-C and apoB 160; risk refined **relative** to the comparison, not cleared |
| 190 mg/dL | 160 mg/dL | 55 mg/dL | Both cholesterol burden and particle count high despite excellent TG |
| 190 mg/dL | Not measured | 55 mg/dL | Particle burden **unknown**; HDL, LDL size guesses and TG/HDL cannot determine apoB |
| 250 mg/dL | High apoB but LDL predominantly “large” | Low | Size phenotype not a basis for declaring particles harmless |

These values are hypothetical, not patient-specific targets. Apply guidelines and lifetime-risk assessment; no single apoB value overrides symptoms, FH, prior ASCVD, age, drugs or family history. [S19, S20]

## 5. When to favor which test

**First-line:** repeat a standard lipid panel with accurately recorded fasting status and diet/weight context; calculate non-HDL-C. For discordance or marked LDL-C, add **apoB** and Lp(a) for a broader assessment. Lp(a) can contribute to measured LDL-C and apoB and may materially change risk interpretation independently of keto. [S16, S19, S20]

**Potential second line:** NMR LDL-P when apoB unavailable or suspected discordance between apoB and LDL-P would actually alter further work-up, when detailed lipoprotein phenotyping is itself a research outcome, or when a specialist is evaluating rare lipid disorders.

**Lower routine priority:** isolated average LDL size, LDL pattern A/B, individual small-LDL subclasses, HDL particle measures, “large LDL safe” interpretation. NMR methods differ; reported subclass bins and units must be checked on each laboratory report. [S23]

**If LDL-C ≥190 mg/dL or apoB ≥140 mg/dL:** the 2026 dyslipidemia guideline frames these as severe hypercholesterolemia with high lifetime risk; evaluate other etiologies (e.g., FH, hypothyroidism, nephrotic pathology), exact diet composition and treatment options under medical supervision. Lipid phenotype labels, TG/HDL ratios and a zero CAC score do **not** provide an exemption. [S20]

## 6. Assay and longitudinal study cautions

1. **Calculated versus direct LDL-C:** Friedewald, Martin/Hopkins, Sampson and direct homogeneous assays can disagree, particularly at extreme triglycerides or unusual lipid composition. Do not ascribe any change to LDL particle size without comparing measurement methods.
2. **Biological variation:** repeat under similar nutritional, fasting, medication and lab conditions; do not infer mechanism from one result.
3. **ApoB vs LDL-P:** do not numerically convert mg/dL apoB to nmol/L LDL-P with a universal factor. They measure related but nonidentical particle populations and methods.
4. **Lp(a):** maintain molar nmol/L units when possible and note assay limitations; elevated Lp(a) makes a separate contribution to apoB and LDL-C. [S16]
5. **Risk outcomes:** observational predictive superiority is not identical to randomized demonstration that ordering an NMR panel improves hard outcomes.
6. **Imaging:** CAC and CCTA reflect detectable calcified/noncalcified plaque at the scan date; a score of zero does not prove unlimited future tolerance of very high apoB. [S13, S20]

## 7. Research agenda for the specific ketogenic TG-low phenotype

A useful study would collect **paired LDL-C, direct LDL-C, apoB, Lp(a), non-HDL-C and NMR LDL-P** in weight-stable ketogenic subjects stratified by LDL-C severity, baseline BMI, body composition, saturated-fat fraction and genetically established FH. Compare against age-, sex-, BMI- and apoB-matched nonketogenic groups. Outcomes should include longitudinal plaque progression and clinical events; mechanistic tracer work should separately study LDL particle **production, clearance and cholesterol cargo**.

This would assess whether the low-TG/high-HDL phenotype changes risk at *equal apoB*, rather than assuming risk disappears because atherogenic dyslipidemia differs from the insulin-resistant phenotype.

**Bottom line:** ApoB is usually the best first advanced addition; NMR LDL-P is a viable, context-dependent supplement. Large LDL particles and favorable triglycerides may describe an unusual metabolic profile, but they do not negate a high concentration of atherogenic particles.
