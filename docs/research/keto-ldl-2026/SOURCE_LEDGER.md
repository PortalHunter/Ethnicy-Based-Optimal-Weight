# Source ledger — ketogenic LDL-C, body recomposition, apoB/NMR and Southeast Asian ancestry

**Evidence cut-off:** 2026-11-05. **Purpose:** standalone citation registry for this research branch; no citations or external text are incorporated into the calculator. **Verification:** primary PubMed abstract, journal page, or open-access article inspected where specified. A DOI/PMID link identifies a publication, not necessarily independent verification of every derived statistic. The studies are not necessarily representative of a ketogenic Filipino population.

## Source-control and correction notes

1. **S16 subgroup inconsistencies require special caution:** 1,927 is the abstract/method total, while Table 1 is marked n=1,928. Table header appears Chinese 1,104 / Malay 247 / Indian 178 / Filipino 325 / others 74, but ethnic percentages in prose imply Malay ~325 and Filipino ~247. **Do not silently force one version.** The median apoB and LDL-C associations are reported in prose and are less sensitive to count labeling, but an author correction/erratum is needed before relying on precise subgroup denominators.
2. **S15 genotype precision:** rs6102059 interaction (P=0.01) applies to a composite dyslipidemia outcome, whereas LDL-specific interaction P=0.28. No validated genotype diet decision rule.
3. **S14 retraction:** never use the retracted longitudinal KETO-CTA article as evidence for no plaque progression or lack of apoB atherogenicity. Keep S13 baseline cross-sectional report clearly separate.
4. **S03 weight stability wording:** “maintaining ≥5% loss from original weight” can coexist with a substantial regain and is not equivalent to 8+ weeks of genuinely unchanged body mass.
5. **S18 cohort denominator wording:** MESA recruited 6,814; the main LDL-C/LDL-P CVD discordance analysis uses 5,598 participants.
6. Check every new claim against its endpoint, comparator, preplanned status, unit, sample count, published errata/retractions and applicability to a true body-recomposition phase.
