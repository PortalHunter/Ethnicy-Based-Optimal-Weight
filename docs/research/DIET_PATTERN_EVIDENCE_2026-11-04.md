# Dietary-pattern evidence review for optional lab discussions

**Reviewed:** 2026-11-04 · **Scope:** general educational discussion of published trials/guidelines · **Status:** source-checked, not a clinical decision algorithm, individual treatment plan, or an endorsement of any dietary personality.

## Design and interpretation

The ideal-weight calculator and its equations are **not affected** by this document. None of the findings below establishes what diet will work for a specific person's lab numbers, ancestry, genotype or weight. Terms such as "keto", "low carbohydrate", "Mediterranean", "DASH", "Portfolio", "plant-forward", and "time-restricted eating" do **not** describe identical interventions. Trial populations, intervention delivery, food quality, calories, duration, adherence, medications, measured outcomes and safety all differ.

This is a **focused primary-source review**, not a systematic review or exhaustive harm assessment. Study findings are average effects (or lack of demonstrated difference), not individual forecasts. User-facing diet comparisons must be optional; unknown/normal reported lab flags cannot trigger condition-specific diet advice. Critical laboratory flags or concerning values suppress food guidance. All wording is explicitly **for a conversation with a clinician or registered dietitian**, particularly for patients taking medicines or with kidney disease.

## Trial and guideline matrix

| Approach | Primary study or authority | Measured finding and population | Interpretation and caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mediterranean-style** | [PREDIMED *republished* trial, NEJM 2018, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa1800389](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1800389), 7,447 high-risk Spanish adults | Mediterranean diet plus olive oil or nuts reduced a composite cardiovascular outcome relative to control in a reanalysis. | [The original 2013 PREDIMED report was retracted and replaced](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc1806491) because some recruitment/randomization was irregular. Use **2018 reanalysis**, never unqualified original claims; interventions were supervised, involved particular foods and population. |
| **Mediterranean-style, established heart disease** | [CORDIOPREV, Lancet 2022, PMID 35525255](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35525255/), 1,002 patients with known coronary disease | At 7 years, 87 composite events in Mediterranean vs 111 in low-fat group; adjusted HR around 0.72–0.75. | Cardiovascular outcomes, not a trial of lab-selected diet personalization; ~82.5% were men and setting was single-site Spain. |
| **Very-low-carbohydrate ketogenic vs low-carb Mediterranean** | [Keto-Med crossover RCT, AJCN 2022, DOI 10.1093/ajcn/nqac154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35641199/), 40 randomized, primary analysis 33 | After 12 weeks per phase, **no significant difference in HbA1c** between diets. Triglyceride percentage change: −16% keto vs −5% Mediterranean; LDL-C: +10% keto vs −5% Mediterranean. Keto phase had lower fiber and some nutrient intake. | Small, short crossover trial in adults with prediabetes or T2D. Outcomes are not long-term cardiovascular-event evidence. Different ketogenic formulations may affect LDL differently; **do not claim keto always raises LDL** or that it reverses diabetes for a particular person. A [2022 correction, DOI 10.1093/ajcn/nqac279](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/nqac279) **only rescales Figure 3's percentage-change y-axis from −0.4…0.2 to −40…20**; the authors report no changes to data points. The primary abstract findings remain as reported. |
| **Low-carbohydrate (not always ketogenic)** | [ADA 2026 Standards, nutrition §5](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S005); [BMJ 2021 meta-analysis, PMID 33441384](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33441384/) | Low-carb patterns can improve glycemia and triglycerides in the short term; effects and adherence vary, and many advantages diminish around 12 months. | Low-carb and **nutritional ketosis must not be conflated**. No fixed universal carbohydrate prescription; carbohydrate quality matters. Some diabetes medications require adjustment *by a clinician*. |
| **DASH and sodium** | [DASH-Sodium feeding trial, NEJM 2001, DOI 10.1056/NEJM200101043440101](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJM200101043440101), 412 participants | Lower blood pressure with DASH and reduced sodium vs controls in controlled feeding; effects in people with and without hypertension. | A blood-pressure outcome, **not** proof that a flag on a cholesterol or A1c report warrants a sodium prescription. Kidney disease, potassium imbalance and medications may change what is safe. |
| **Portfolio diet** | [Chiavaroli et al., 2018 meta-analysis, PMID 29807048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29807048/), 7 comparisons, 439 people with hyperlipidemia | LDL-C fell on average −0.73 mmol/L (95% CI −0.89 to −0.56), roughly 17% vs comparator. No significant body-weight change. | Small trials, LDL is a surrogate endpoint; do not claim this intervention alone demonstrates fewer heart attacks or changes ideal weight. Core components include plant protein, viscous fiber, nuts and plant sterols. |
| **Quality-focused plant-forward / healthy low-fat** | [DIETFITS RCT, JAMA 2018, DOI 10.1001/jama.2018.0245](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29466592/), 609 adults without diabetes | 12-month weight change −5.3 kg healthy low-fat vs −6.0 kg healthy low-carb; not a statistically significant difference. No confirmed interaction for the tested three-SNP genotype pattern or baseline insulin secretion. | Both groups received intensive counseling. **No validated genotype-to-diet rule** arises from this trial. Plant-forward patterns can be adapted to cultural staples; do not infer suitability from ancestry. |
| **Time-restricted eating / intermittent fasting** (timing, not a complete diet) | [Liu et al., NEJM 2022, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2114833](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35443107/), 139 participants with obesity | At 12 months, 8-hour eating-window plus energy restriction vs continuous restriction produced no statistically significant weight-loss difference (−8.0 vs −6.3 kg; between-group P=.11). | Selected groups can benefit from structure, but this trial did **not** show fasting is universally superior. ADA 2026 warns about insulin/secretagogue hypoglycemia during fasting; coordinate medications and timing with a clinician. |
| **Cardiovascular dietary-pattern alignment** | [AHA 2023 scientific statement, PMID 37128940](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37128940/) | Mediterranean, DASH, pescetarian and vegetarian patterns align well with AHA dietary guidance; very-low-carb/keto and paleo align poorly under criteria used (including saturated fat, fiber and food restrictions). | An alignment assessment, not a direct clinical-outcomes RCT or proof every formulation of keto is harmful. |

## Decision framework for Ancestry Health

**Educational candidates:**
- If a **user-reported high HbA1c** or **user-reported high fasting plasma glucose with confirmed fasting** is entered, mention Mediterranean-style and quality-focused lower-carbohydrate patterns for discussion; note that restrictive keto differs from moderate low-carb and has safety/LDL considerations.
- If **LDL-C is reported high**, point to Mediterranean and Portfolio as studied options; do not recommend ketogenic restriction as an LDL treatment, and acknowledge that some keto formulations raise LDL.
- If **non-severe triglycerides are reported high**, mention general carbohydrate-quality strategies and consider a discussion of lower-carb vs Mediterranean, explicitly noting the Keto-Med lipid tradeoff. **Severe triglycerides require clinician-led evaluation**, not an app food plan.
- DASH is appropriate as a **general evidence overview for blood pressure**, not a response to lab markers our app does not measure. The Diet Research Library may show it; the lab-result notes should not pretend blood pressure was entered.
- Time-restricted eating is a **timing strategy**, not a substitute for food quality, and requires medication/clinical review where appropriate.
- No ancestry-based diet-ranking, SNP response assignment, individualized macronutrient goal, medication change, automatic elimination diet, supplement regimen or claim of diabetes remission.

**Red flags for strict keto / fasting**: ADA 2026 explicitly discourages ketogenic eating among SGLT2-inhibitor users at DKA risk and advises against very-low-carb patterns in pregnancy/lactation, children, kidney disease, or those with or at risk of disordered eating; people using insulin or hypoglycemia-causing secretagogues require medical oversight for restricted carbs/fasting. The current app does not collect sufficient medication, disease, symptom or nutrition history to establish eligibility. **Avoid suggesting keto as a self-start intervention based on a lab value.**

**Cultural usability**: describe recognizable *food characteristics* rather than asserting an ethnicity-specific diet. Legumes, grains, leafy vegetables and unsaturated fats can come from many culinary traditions; vegetarian, religious, affordability and accessibility constraints matter.

## Reviewing Dr. Eric Berg and other public diet claims

[Eric Berg's own biography](https://www.drberg.com/about-dr-berg/dr-berg-bio) identifies him as a **Doctor of Chiropractic (D.C.)** and promoter of a proprietary *Healthy Keto®* approach and intermittent fasting. His talks are **claims to investigate**, not independent trials demonstrating the effects of his particular plan. We should evaluate each specific, date-stamped claim (e.g., "fasting reverses insulin resistance" or "ketogenic diets are best for all metabolic markers") by comparing its supporting papers, study designs, controls and contrary evidence—including Keto-Med, DIETFITS, ADA 2026 and the AHA statement. Do **not** use a personality's popularity, credentials or commercial products as an evidence grade or promote branded supplements.

A Berg-specific or other creator-specific claim can be added to a **separate reviewed claim log** with verbatim quote under fair-use limits, source date/URL, primary citations, outcome, clinical population, contradictions, financial interests, and conclusion (supported / partially supported / unsupported / not assessable). A secondary description of their claims must **not** be silently rephrased as demonstrated clinical fact.

## Publication gate

Before suggesting a dietary option, cite a source relevant to the **specified biomarker**, state the evidence design, provide a potential downside and advise discussing suitability with the patient's clinician or dietitian. Source verification here does not authorize clinical personal-diet selection. Qualitative education may be shown on the public, noncommercial site; numeric outcomes must never be presented as predicted changes for that visitor.
