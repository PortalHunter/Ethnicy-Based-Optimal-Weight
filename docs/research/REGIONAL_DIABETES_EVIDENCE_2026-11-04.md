# Regional diabetes research for ancestry-aware education (November 4, 2026)

**Status:** Research-scoped educational reference mapping, not a systematic review or validated clinical-decision algorithm. Local-language source discovery and source-identity checks have been performed, but this register is **not** a claim-by-claim independent full-text medical appraisal. Nothing here changes BMI cutoffs, the original diabetes-related weight calculation, medication decisions, diagnosis, or laboratory thresholds.

## Motivation and boundaries

When someone selects the existing combined **Type 2 diabetes / prediabetes / family history** checkbox and calculates a result, the website can show a **separate source-linked education panel** for each *explicitly selected* ancestry or regional background. The original checkbox does not distinguish personally diagnosed diabetes, prediabetes or family history; **do not infer which applies**. Better segmentation of this option is a future clinical-design task.

Ethnicity, ancestry, a genetically verified variant, country of residence, and the population covered by a clinical guideline are different concepts. A guideline produced for residents in Japan or Ghana does not automatically apply to everyone of Japanese or West African ancestry. Research from more geographically isolated/founder populations is valuable, but ancestry selection still never proves genotype or individual disease probability.

A broad picker may include many distinct communities. For a source from only one member country or single clinic, label the country/setting explicitly, **never** pretend it covers the entire broad option. For unmapped backgrounds, say the source review is incomplete; do not invent a risk estimate or borrow a neighboring country's guidance without labelling the mismatch. For mixed selections, display references independently and **never** blend claims or use the entered percentages as evidence weights.

## Missing and future regional review

**Do not silently claim coverage for all 62 subgroup choices.** Important source-review gaps include South Asian populations beyond India and Pakistan, Central/East Africa and smaller African communities, Balkan and Baltic countries, many Caribbean groups, Central and Andean America, Inuit nations outside Greenland, Pacific Micronesian/Melanesian peoples, and several distinct Southeast Asian countries. Local medical literature in Arabic, French, Amharic, Bangla, Tagalog, Khmer, Lao, Portuguese, Spanish and other languages should be actively sought. Record each study's original title and an English reviewer translation.

Priorities for expert collaboration:
1. Independently verify publication versions, original text, ethics and authorship, sample characteristics, endpoints, statistical uncertainty and differences between guideline editions.
2. Search for reliable national/ethnic-subpopulation studies that directly involve the requested population, not merely an adjacent country.
3. Resolve conflicting evidence, ecological fallacy, ancestry-versus-residency errors, health-system access and environmental factors.
4. Keep [issue #2](https://github.com/PortalHunter/Ethnicy-Based-Optimal-Weight/issues/2) separate: original diabetes-related weight ceiling and family-history conflation remain unresolved.
5. Seek qualified domain review before publishing individualized screening thresholds or changing formulas; present the research panel strictly as educational references.

## Interface acceptance criteria

- Without the diabetes checkbox, the regional evidence panel is hidden.
- With diabetes selected and valid form results, show relevant sources only for the **explicitly selected** subgroup(s) plus specific coverage gaps for other selections.
- In mixed heritage mode, show each chosen background independently, with no weighting or genotype inference.
- In single broad-category mode, admit where no local subgroup source has yet been reviewed rather than presenting a random country's guideline as universally applicable.
- An invalid form clears the population research panel with the other results.
- Changing the diabetes checkbox or ancestry after calculation refreshes the panel.
- No new data are stored or transmitted; links open sources externally.
- The shared calculation core and original v9 archive stay untouched.
