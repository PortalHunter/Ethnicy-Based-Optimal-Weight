## Regionally balanced sampling

Do not rely solely on English-language search or the unusually accessible Irish archive. A 100-attestation pilot target can include 20 Ireland/Northern Europe, 20 Asia and Pacific, 20 Africa and Middle East, 20 Americas, 20 from other/diaspora/cross-region or underrepresented collections, but **these are recruitment targets, not demographic prevalence claims**. Rebalance when archives restrict access or uneven survival limits comparability.

For each motif, search:
- Endonyms, historical spellings, multiple romanizations, original-script terms, colonial-era labels with caution, and names for related entities or symptoms.
- First source edition; additional dated editions; oral recording or transcript; local and regional re-tellings; translated copies; depictions contradicted in neighboring regions.
- Explicit date and place, actual narrator and collector, manuscript folio / archival call number, copyright and community access.
- Related academic literature separately. Never promote a modern blog quoting an encyclopedia as an independent historical account.

## Initial motif search dictionary (research search hints, not diagnoses)

- Night paralysis, chest pressure, nightmare: `bangungot`, `batibat`, `pisadeira`, `kanashibari`, `old hag`, `pandafeche`, `karabasan`, `mara`.
- Child illness or unexplained developmental changes: `changeling`, `fairy child`, `fairy stroke` (do not retrospectively pathologize families).
- Unexplained misfortune or symptoms: `evil eye`, `mal de ojo`, `nazar`, `susto`; investigate distinct traditions on their own terms, not as synonyms.
- Traditional cures: `whooping cough`, `chincough`, `jaundice`, `toothache`, `warts`, `herbal cure`, `fairy doctor`.
- Environmental and animal warnings: `mice illness`, `rodent taboos`, `seasonal disease`; protect indigenous knowledge.
- Cultural concepts that may impact medicine: `lihi`, `pasma`, `namamana`, `koro`, `ataque de nervios`.

## Distinguishing three kinds of evidence

**1. Historical attestation:** supports "this person/collector/source recorded this account" only.

**2. Cross-cultural resemblance:** supports "these accounts share narrated features" only, subject to independent-source and translation checks.

**3. Biomedical evidence:** requires independently valid cohort/clinical/mechanistic data and a credible comparator. This is *not provided* by folklore.
