# ATLAS — historical folklore × modern medical science: pilot protocol

**Proposed on:** 2026-11-19  
**Status:** discovery pilot **prepared, not yet clinically run or validated**  
**Workspace:** isolated folklore research branch; no changes to calculator, medical evidence integration, or A/B/D team branches  
**Source manifest:** [2026-11-19 inventory: 1,013 mixed records](../intake/catalog-manifest-2026-11-19.json)  
**Initial seed queue:** [22 linked source records](seed-candidates-2026-11-19.csv)

## Decision: Is 1,013 enough?

**YES** for a **small, exploratory, structured comparison** that tests whether reliable historical descriptions can be mapped to questions worth investigating using modern medical literature.

**NO** for epidemiological prevalence, sensitivity/specificity, incidence of a disease in any population, genotype-risk mapping, efficacy of a ritual or remedy, or evidence that a fictional character had a diagnosed disorder.

The number 1,013 is **metadata rows**, including book titles, manuscript pages, within-one-book passages, secondary scholarly articles, alternate spellings and probable reprints. In the November 19 manifest these occupy **113 distinct source URL strings**, which themselves are not synonymous with distinct original witnesses. Independent narrators and original-language source versions are *not yet reliably counted*. Therefore no meaningful statistical power calculation can use the 1,013 figure.

## Controlled pilot design

- **Screening phase:** 22 prioritized seed records, explicitly including **2 modern scientific/literature bridges** and **6 literary/legendary negative controls**. Remaining historical source leads are **not** assumed independent.
- **First comparative study target:** roughly **30–40 source-located textual units** after deduplication and source access review, deliberately including **8–12 negative controls**. This is a pragmatic feasibility target, *not* a calculated or representative sample size.
- **Focus areas:** German nocturnal pressure; historical fainting/apparent death; Irish `falling sickness`; nocturnal sudden-death concept vs nightmare; fever/`ague` beliefs; Malay spirit/soul and postpartum narratives; animal fables and magical cures as controls.
- **Selection:** stratify by narrative genre, place, century, original language/translation, first-hand vs retelling, and whether physiological features actually appear in the *text*, not just a summary. Prefer historically independent first-hand descriptions; cap influence of a single compiler; select nonmedical controls before analysis.
- **Separation of labor:** folklorist/translator authenticates text and genre; independent research assistant extracts descriptive features **without seeing candidate modern diagnoses**; clinician with relevant specialization compares to current science; historian reviews anachronism; community representative oversees Indigenous/restricted materials. Record reviewer disagreements.
- **Count units separately:** (a) metadata records, (b) unique tales/chapters, (c) editions, (d) independent tellers and transmission chains, (e) documented lived experiences, (f) source-backed biomedical hypotheses. Do not mix any of these denominators.

1. Sleep paralysis and cross-cultural interpretation: [Sleep Paralysis: Neurophysiology and Culture (2023)](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/full/10.1176/appi.ajp-rj.2023.180401); [Sleep paralysis review (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12349844/).
2. Apparent blackouts, syncope and seizure differential: [NICE guidance on transient loss of consciousness](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg109/chapter/Recommendations); [Evaluation and management of acute transient loss of consciousness (2025)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39893024/).
3. Sudden unexplained nocturnal death in the Philippines: [Bangungut and Brugada hypothesis review (2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3404490/); [SUNDS autopsy case-control study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4832694/). Note that a hypothesis about a *subset* of SUNDS must not become a diagnosis for every Bangungot narrative.
4. Historical retrospective diagnostic limitations: [Retrospective diagnosis and use of historical texts (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29539322/); [Retrospective diagnosis: process, outputs and relevance (2026)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20542704261483853).

All external scientific sources still need formal evidence appraisal when used for a finished review. Do not use unreviewed abstracts alone to assert clinical mechanisms.

## Two-stage extraction / review fields

**Historical text layer** (before any medical mapping):
`atlas_record_id`, `verified_source_locator`, `original_publication_date`, `teller_date`, `language`, `teller_provenance`, `rights_community_gate`, `genre`, `literality_of_report`, `independence_group_id`, `original_phrasing_if_lawful`, `translation_uncertainty`, `episode_timing`, `described_sensory_motor_experiences`, `observable_vs_attributed_event`, `reported_outcome`, `contradictions`.

**Medical comparison layer** (only after text layer approved):
`modern_clinical_question`, `sourced_scientific_reference`, `evidence_quality`, `plausible_mechanisms`, `alternative_medical_explanations`, `nonmedical_and_literary_alternatives`, `distinguishing_features_missing`, `disconfirming_evidence`, `narrative_dependency_concern`, `cultural_appropriateness_review`, `grade`, `reviewer_ids`, `reviewer_disagreement`, `next_research_action`.

**Grade meanings** (never probabilities):
- **A — Literature already characterizes the *modern phenomenon*.** Historical story parallel is only a **resemblance**, unless actual features established.
- **B — Historical account has concrete descriptive features and independently verified documentary provenance; plausible alternatives remain.**
- **C — Story or term resembles an illness but is a heavily edited, derivative or symbolic text; historically suggestive only.**
- **D — Supernatural fiction, source too vague, copyright/permission barrier or no interpretable physiological observation.**

No grade says “the tale proves a disease.” Keep negative and contradictory cases visible.

## Stopping rules and quality metrics

1. **Provenance/rights stop:** if source inaccessible, protected, culturally restricted, unsigned or unsafe to reproduce, keep only permitted link metadata and mark review blocked; do not silently discard.
2. **Clinical inference stop:** no condition assignment without independently documented signs and verified context; symptoms in a fairy tale do not meet clinical criteria.
3. **Duplicate stop:** merge *analysis weight*, not underlying editions, when one author reprints or quotes another; record the separate versions without claiming additional corroboration.
4. **Premature-success stop:** compare extraction accuracy and false illness suggestion rate on negative controls; if unreliable, improve the rubric before scanning hundreds more titles.
5. **Discovery metrics:** proportion of sources actually retrievable; attestation independence ratio; percent with trustworthy original-language passage; reviewer agreement; fraction yielding concrete *testable* questions; false-medical-signal rate on negative controls; fraction blocked by permissions; time per complete comparison.
6. **Sampling limitation:** convenience/curated selection creates selection bias. Do not report general-population disease prevalence, ancestry differences or Bayesian disease likelihoods from this sample.

## Deliverable of the first pilot

A table of 20–30 **source-verified**, culturally suitable historical narrative units (or the maximum available with explicit reasons for shortfall), a control set of 8–12 nonmedical/magical tales, and a **5–8-page narrative synthesis** of supported scientific parallels *and failed comparisons*. Distinguish:
- *Observational concordance* — wording that resembles an independently studied modern phenomenon;
- *Hypothesis to test* — a discriminating scientific question needing new data;
- *Historical/cultural insight* — a valid result even when no medical inference is supportable;
- *Literary negative control* — evidence the system correctly rejects magical or trivial similarities.

Any possible health claim must proceed through a separate clinician-led Atlas review. This pilot is **not** the clinical review itself.
