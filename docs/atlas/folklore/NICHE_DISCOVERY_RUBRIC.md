# Niche-signal triage and variant-preserving research protocol (v0.1)

**Scope:** exploratory historical/ethnographic research, not clinical evidence or patient advice. Created 2026-11-18. Apply only after archival provenance and culturally appropriate permission checks.

## Why rarity alone is not a priority score

A rare story might document an unusual symptom, but rarity may simply reflect missing archives, translation, selection bias, social stigma, storytelling invention or a solitary derivative text. Avoid a system that rewards the strangest legend with the strangest medical diagnosis. Treat highly specific *documented features* as leads to questions, not answers.

## Mandatory gates (failure → quarantine/restrict, no numeric score)

- **Source identity:** exact book and version, archive page or manuscript call number, edition/recording date, location, original language and collection pathway. Mark unknown, don't invent.
- **Rights, cultural stewardship and harm:** determine link-only/public domain/permission-required; no replication of sensitive or restricted oral traditions; consult relevant local experts. Do not publish private informant identifiers or traditional knowledge that a community considers restricted.
- **Witness classification:** oral eyewitness, firsthand ethnography, firsthand collector's transcription, retelling, school essay, printed fairy tale, academic interpretation, anonymous web repost, or index-only label.
- **Edition dependency:** translation, quotation, adaptation, reprint, shared publisher or uncertain relationship recorded as an edge; two URLs do not guarantee two witnesses.
- **Literalness:** classify observed human experience vs genre, supernatural interpretation, ritual rule, allegory, satire, miracle, origin story, social norm, or fictional character. Distinguish described effect from a believed cause.

## Optional discovery score (0–15); never a clinical probability

Score five 0–3 axes, each with evidence and reviewer:
- **Source quality (0–3):** clear source, dated original, credible provenance and documented uncertainty.
- **Phenotype / experience specificity (0–3):** temporality, identifiable signs, exposure sequence, reported outcome; separate narrativized fantasy from experience.
- **Independence of attestations (0–3):** corroborated only where distinct chains of transmission established; repeated citations are 0.
- **Discriminating testability (0–3):** competing explanations yield distinguishable predictions with external evidence and plausible testing methods; reduce for unfalsifiable proposals.
- **Ethical/research feasibility (0–3):** respectful, lawful and non-invasive access to enough context; zero when community permission prohibits use.

Use **rarity as an audit dimension, not scoring points**. Include an evidence-unavailable state. Require a null hypothesis and at least one explicit nonmedical or literary-diffusion account. Record reviewer disagreement.

## Workflow for each candidate

1. Capture the exact *attestation*, not a generalized "traditional belief." Preserve bibliography and page/folio, original term, transcription and translation, collector/narrator (if lawful), and date recorded vs date claimed.
2. Build a source genealogy graph: direct quotations, editions, reprints, translated derivatives and independently reported variants. Never collapse conflicting details.
3. Blindly extract descriptive signal phrases into fields: setting/exposure, onset, duration, sensory or motor signs, behavior, lay explanation, intervention, reported outcome, alternative features, uncertainty. **Do not insert a diagnosis at this stage.**
4. A folklorist/linguist checks cultural context, translation, symbolic function, and genre. Community expertise is not optional where restricted or appropriated knowledge may be involved.
5. In a separate document, ask if the descriptive pattern already has an explanation, which alternatives fit, and what would disconfirm the biomedical idea. Cross-check modern epidemiology, clinical studies, ecology, archival public-health reports or historical disease records.
6. Report the pattern's independent-documentation count (not number of websites or retellings) and contradictory observations. If the story is a magic fairy tale, label it as such.
7. Keep all outputs in hypothesis queue. No operational prescriptions, ancestral disease labels, medical decisions or calculator influence absent separate expert clinical evidence review.

## Examples selected for falsifiability, not diagnosis

| Archival motif | Preserve as reported | Competing explanations to explore, *not claims* | Specific red flag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bavarian `Alpdrücken` and Mecklenburg `Mor-riden` | Nighttime pressure, immobility, subjective presence in 19th-century printed accounts | Sleep-related physiology, historical descriptive convention, textual borrowing, anxiety, fictional supernatural narrative | Terms may describe different local genres; no patient confirmation |
| Irish `falling sickness`, ague, local cures | Local names for an illness, claimed attacks/remedies, manuscript context | Broad historical differential and linguistic shifts, narrative exaggeration, healer reputation, other environmental factors | No clinical definition and potentially dangerous remedies |
| Malay `semangat` and `penanggalan` | The attributed soul/spirit causal explanation and described experiences | Social and ritual model, genre transmission, postpartum interpretations if source supports them | Skeat quoted earlier `Hikayat Abdullah`; derivative text is not independent |
| Lao `Thao Kamphra Kaikaew` | Magical snakebite/unconsciousness/revival in a published story | Fantasy motif, narrative borrowing, known physiology only if non-fiction supporting source emerges | Fictional resurrection cannot be assessed as a medical observation |
| Borneo blian ritual account | A travel writer observed healer practice around an illness | Ritual/social support, natural recovery, environmental exposures, observation bias | A claimed cure does not establish effectiveness |

## Calibration against negative controls

- Sample fables with talking animals, impossible magical transformations, cosmic origin stories and comedic exaggeration. Do not turn every striking description into a diagnosis.
- Insert known textual duplicates; model should identify dependence rather than claim independent corroboration.
- Human-review original language and translation disagreements.
- Measure false medical discovery rate against control tales; audit chance findings from many thousands of texts and selective reporting.
- Report both `no medical feature found` and `promising but no independent evidence`. These are successes in disciplined screening, not failures.

## Priority additions

- Original Irish manuscript vs school transcription; historic terms `ague`, `falling sickness`, `consumption` and `cancer` have historically broad meanings.
- German regional story versions `Alpdrücken`, `Mort-riden` in different compilations and years.
- Malay Skeat 1900 references vs `Hikayat Abdullah` 1849 original Jawi; determine whether one quotes the other.
- Lao `Twelve Sisters` 2023 comparative study references seven narratives (4 Thai / 1 each Cambodian, Lao, Burmese); recover individual texts/collectors, never count referenced versions without attestation.
- Philippine 1908 Journal of American Folklore medical-practice note; seek original journal pagination and narrator/translator provenance.
- Borneo Lumholtz chXIII 1920 edition reporting 1913–17 travels; disentangle first-hand report, local quotation and later interpretation.
