Ancestry-Adjusted Optimal Body Weight & Food Calculator

Estimates a healthy weight range for adults (18+) from ancestry-specific BMI cut-offs (WHO 2004, NICE), adjusted for frame size from your wrist and, optionally, elbow, and adds food notes for selected conditions and ancestry-linked traits. Every claim links to its source.

 






Measurement tips: use a soft tape; measure the wrist at its narrowest point just beyond the wrist bone (toward the hand) (MedlinePlus). Elbow breadth is the width between the two bony points of the elbow with the arm bent at 90°, ideally with a caliper; a tape against a ruler is less accurate. Errors of a tenth of an inch can change the frame result near a cut-off.

About these numbers. This is an educational estimate, not medical advice. BMI cannot tell muscle from fat, and the cut-offs are less reliable for very muscular people, people 65 and over, and during pregnancy (do not use it if pregnant). Ancestry cut-offs and gene notes describe population averages, not you personally. Frame size from a tape measure is approximate, and the ideal-weight formulas date from mid-20th-century US data. For these reasons the page gives ranges, not a single "perfect" weight. If you have a medical condition, check any weight or diet change with your doctor or a registered dietitian.
Limitations and how this calculator works
  • Healthy range: BMI 18.5 up to the population's overweight cut-off (25 under WHO international cut-offs; 23 for groups NICE and WHO 2004 assign lower thresholds). The upper edge shown is 0.1 below the cut-off.
  • Frame size: wrist uses the height/wrist ratio cut-offs (Grant 1980, cited via secondary sources); elbow uses the 1983 Metropolitan Life table (heights in 1-inch heels). When the two disagree, both frames are kept and the range widens.
  • Suggested range: the overlap between the four classic ideal-body-weight formulas (Hamwi with ±10% for frame, Devine, Robinson, Miller) and the healthy range. This combination is this calculator's own method, not a clinical standard.
  • Mixed ancestry: the overweight and obesity cut-offs are blended in proportion to the percentages you enter (e.g., 50% in a lower-cut-off group gives 24 / 28.8). No guideline defines cut-offs for mixed ancestry, so this blend is the calculator's own method; the standard and lower ranges are shown alongside it.
  • Conditions: diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol and gout each move the suggested range slightly lower and narrower (calculator weighting, kept inside the healthy range). If you are above the healthy range, the page also shows the 5-7% weight-loss goal from diabetes-prevention guidelines.
  • Not covered: under-18s, pregnancy, eating disorders, and medical weight targets set by your own clinician, which take priority.
