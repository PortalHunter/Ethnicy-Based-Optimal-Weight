How to read this research

Guideline screening

A professional group's criteria for defined populations, years and jurisdictions. Not a medical diagnosis or personal ideal weight.

Population observation

Associations and prevalence from specific cohorts. Neither a deterministic genetic mechanism nor your predicted probability.

Experimental or not actionable

Unvalidated hypotheses or calculator heuristics must not affect treatment, medication, or personal weight targets.

Research provenance is retained in the integration decision log and each team's original evidence matrix. Evidence grades in those documents are research-team judgments, not independent certification.

1. Ancestry and metabolic screening

Key conclusion: More specific heritage categories can improve how research populations are described, but do not establish new Scandinavian, Mediterranean, Mexican or Brazilian “optimal BMI” cutoffs. Keep measured BMI, waist circumference and waist-to-height ratio conceptually separate from risk prediction and treatment goals. Team 1 review · ANC evidence matrix.

Research taxonomy — descriptive, not numeric

European regional backgrounds

Research descriptions may distinguish Nordic/Scandinavian (while separately respecting Finnish and Sámi contexts), Mediterranean Europe, Central Europe, Eastern Europe, Baltic regions and the Balkans. There is no validated region-specific correction that should alter someone's BMI threshold or ideal weight merely from these labels. ANC-006–008 and recommendations.

Central America, South America and Caribbean backgrounds

Mexican, Central American, Caribbean, Andean, Brazilian and Southern Cone descriptions can be recorded without turning nationality into genomic percentages. Afro-Latin identity and Indigenous American nations require respectful nonexclusive options. Cohort differences can reflect migration, structural exposures, lifestyle and access to care; no universal ancestry adjustment follows. ANC-010–020.

Mixed background, screening and jurisdiction

Guideline decision criteria, self-identification, family history and DNA estimates are not interchangeable. NICE UK and ADA US approaches must retain their own labels. A percentage-blended cutoff is an experimental calculator method, not a guideline. The existing calculator's automatic ancestry-based diabetes weight cap is also not a validated treatment target. ANC-024–027: priority corrections.

Practical message: A BMI in a conventional “normal” range does not rule out elevated blood pressure, glucose abnormalities or lipid disorders. Assessments require clinical context rather than automatic ethnic adjustment.

2. Hereditary disease and individual dietary response

Key conclusion: Verified diagnosis, actual phenotype and relevant laboratory findings can be clinically important; family history alone cannot establish a disease or determine a specialized diet. Team 2 review · NUT evidence matrix.

3. Pharmacogenetics and population differences in drug response

Key conclusion: Pharmacogenetics uses specific measured variants and validated context—not assumed ethnicity—to interpret some medication responses. CPIC often advises how to act when a genotype is already known; that is different from whether everyone should undergo pre-treatment testing. Team 3 review · PGX evidence matrix.

Research quality warning: An allele's population frequency is not an individual's genotype, a carrier rate or an adverse-event probability. Panel incompleteness and research underrepresentation can cause inequitable interpretations. Any future clinical module requires independently reviewed phenotype assignments, assay validation and medical oversight. Population evidence · Regulatory/clinical roadmap.

4. Cross-team implementation rules

ANC, NUT and PGX research are peers. A claim from one stream cannot silently alter another stream's calculations or clinical conclusions. Cross-references are evidence links, not algorithmic dependencies. Release of user-facing medical recommendations requires a qualified reviewer, source/version verification, human approval, explicit intended use and a prospective validation plan where necessary.

No-go until approved: ancestry-percentage personal BMI targets, numeric ethnic diabetes weight ceilings, family-history genetic diagnosis, automated therapeutic diet changes, medication selection/doses, and genomic uploads. This page makes no such calculations.
